The game at a glance

Who sits at the table, what everyone starts with, how a month runs from planning to the month end, which clocks run and which wait for you, and how the game decides who wins.

Players and seats In the game

A game seats 2 to 6 players. Each seat is either a person with a phone (or a laptop) or a computer player. The table needs at least one person, because only a person can host it, so a game has at most 5 computer players.

  • People join from their own phone with the room's four-letter code or its QR. Each picks a name (up to 16 characters), a colour and a species.
  • The host is the first person at the table who is connected. The host adds computer players, removes seats, closes the room and presses Start. If the host's phone drops, the next connected person hosts until it comes back.
  • Computer players are added by the host: The Accountant, The Baron, The Grudge, The Gambler and The Good Neighbour, in that order, each with its own temperament. All play at the Trader skill level. With a Claude key on the server they can think and chat with Claude on a small budget. See Computer players.
  • The big screen (a TV or laptop) is not a seat. It shows the public view and has no controls. See The table and the phones.

Once the host presses Start the seats are fixed. Nobody new can join a running game; a player whose phone reloads gets their own seat back, and a player can move their seat to another device with a one-time code.

Planning In the game

Planning is the month's calm moment, with no clock. Everyone uses their phone's hub at once:

  • Order a geo drone for 60 cr. It surveys up to 2 free or listed plots from orbit, and its results arrive as the land grant starts, in time to choose.
  • Sell a plot or a MULE: list it for the land auction with a minimum price. Each player may list up to 2 lots a month, and the land auction takes at most 4 lots a month from everyone together.
  • Talk and read the market: chat and whispers, the store's prices, the export ship's next call.

Colony and council steps in planning

With the council on (the server's default), planning carries the colony's politics, in this order:

  1. Election day in months 1, 4, 7 and 10: candidates announce with a tax promise, everyone votes in secret, and the TV and every phone play the reveal. Today one office is elected, the Governor. A Governor who has served 2 terms in a row may not run again until someone else has held it.
  2. The law vote, when the Governor proposed a law the month before: yes or no, in secret, then the reveal.
  3. Planning proper: the Governor sets the sales tax (0 to 15 %, at most 5 points from the month's starting rate), may propose a law for next month, commission a building, pay off strikers; anyone may donate to the building being funded.

As planning closes, the game records whether the Governor kept their tax promise. Every stage of the votes waits for everyone's Done, with no clock; only the reveals are timed shows. See Colony and politics for the rules of each.

The land grant In the game

Every free plot lights up once, in reading order: the top row from left to right, then the next row. Each lit plot stays lit for 0.65 s. The first player to press Claim while a plot is lit takes it. Each seat may claim 1 plot a month. A player who wants nothing presses "I'm ready" (done with land) and claims no more this month.

The grant ends as soon as every seat has claimed or is done, when no free land is left, or when the light has passed every free plot once. Once every person is done, the light races on: the computer players' presses resolve at once on each lit plot, and the light moves every 0.08 s, so the TV shows the rest of the pass in a few seconds. A phone that drops in the grant does not end it for anyone; it just does not claim.

A press counts for the plot the player saw lit. Computer players press after a human reaction time, so a quick person can beat them.

Example

In month 1 of a four-seat game, the 15 × 9 map has 134 free plots. One full pass of the light would take 134 × 0.65 s = 87.1 s. The pass is usually much shorter: as soon as all four seats have claimed their one plot, the grant ends.

Why it is like this

One claim a month: Heiko, 5 Nov ("we should only have 1"). The store's MULE supply is sized separately, so cutting the claims did not shrink the corral. One pass with no clock: Heiko approved on 28 Oct that the grant always completes one full pass over the free plots instead of running on a fixed 60 s clock, so a player who wants no land does not hold up the table.

The land auction In the game

After the grant, each month there is a 10 % chance that the colony puts one free plot up for sale, at a minimum of two thirds of a plot's Worth. With nothing listed by players or the colony, the land auction is skipped and development starts at once.

Otherwise the lots come up one at a time: a 3 s intro, a 12 s walk in which bidders hold Up, and a 2.5 s result. A change of leader in the last 2 s puts the clock back to 2 s, at most 6 s more per lot. The highest bid at the bell wins; the store buys what does not sell.

Development and its clock In the game

Development is the arcade part of the month. Every colonist starts at the store, and everyone steers theirs live at once: buy a MULE at the corral, fit it at an outfitter, lead it out and install it on your plot. You can also analyse ground (8 s on the plot), fit a filter (1 Alloy), re-outfit a MULE, fight wolves, race for a glint, or go to the pub.

One clock for everyone

There is one development clock of 60 s, the same for every player, shown on the TV and on every phone. It counts down from the start in every month, month 1 included. Hunger and sickness never take time off the clock; they slow your walk instead:

  • Hunger: a player who starts development short of this month's Food walks slower, from 60 % of normal pace with no Food at all, rising evenly to full pace with all of it.
  • Sickness: each development a player may fall sick (likelier in dirty air and for a big polluter). A sick player walks at 50 % pace. One Medicine held when the turn starts cures it and is used up.
  • The two multiply: no Food and sick is 0.6 × 0.5 = 30 % pace. A Zipwing's × 1.25 comes on top.

Development ends when the clock runs out, or earlier once every player, computer players included, is at the pub or out of time (or has dropped), and no colonist is still walking. A MULE still on your lead when your turn ends runs away; one you took off your own plot goes back to it, and one you swapped off a plot goes back to the store's corral.

The pub

Walking into the pub ends your turn and pays cash: more the more time you have left, and more later in the game. The pub pays 20 + (60 + 20 × month) × the share of the clock left.

Example

In month 3, Lena installs her MULE and walks into the pub with 45 s of the 60 s left. Her share is 45 / 60 = 0.75, so the pub pays 20 + (60 + 3 × 20) × 0.75 = 20 + 120 × 0.75 = 110 cr. A player who walks straight to the pub in month 1 with the full clock gets 20 + 80 = 100 cr; in month 12, 20 + 300 = 320 cr.

Why it is like this

Heiko, 28 Oct: "if we just steer it via the walking then managing the time bars also gets easier. Then we only have one global time bar for all users, which is much better." The PM then ruled that every per-player condition acts through walking pace, never the clock. The clock became 60 s on 4 Nov (Heiko: "lets just generally increase the time to 60 seconds"). The hunger floor is Heiko's ("Yes, 60%", 4 Nov). Month 1 counts down like any month since 5 Nov (Heiko: "Yes, always count"); it used to wait for everyone's first MULE.

What is changing

The old tap-to-walk development screen is being removed so every game steers (PR #369, the Auctions team, being built). Steered walking is already the default in every game the server hosts.

Production In the game

Every plot with a MULE makes its good; nobody acts, everyone watches. The phase lasts exactly as long as its show: a 1 s intro, a 1.1 s beat for each good made (every player's plots of that good together), all bonuses for 1.6 s, all losses for 1.6 s, the month's production events (at most 30 s), then the flight into the silos and a 2 s hold.

Once every person at the table has pressed Speed up, the rest of the show plays 3 times faster. Computer players always agree; a dropped phone is not waited for. After the show, each player can press "Ready for the auction".

Example

A quiet month in which plots made Food, Energy and Smithore: 1 s intro + 3 × 1.1 s + 0.5 s gap + 1.6 s + 1.6 s + 0.5 s gap + 2.4 s of flights + 0.75 s for the last flight + 2 s hold = 13.65 s. The longest possible month, all nine goods and events at their 30 s cap, comes to 50.75 s.

Why it is like this

Heiko, 5 Nov, on the production mock: harvest "resource by resource for all players in parallel", then the bonuses for everyone, then the losses, "and the way that the resources fly to the silos is super". The PM ruled the same day that Ready waits for the end of the show and Speed up is the only shortcut, following his "more dramatic" ask.

The goods auction In the game

Auction events strike first (a pirate raid or a store fire), the store posts its prices, and the goods come up one at a time: Food, Water, Energy, Smithore, Crystite, Rare earth, Alloy, Electronics, Medicine. A good nobody can trade is skipped. For each good:

  1. Side pick on a status screen of what everyone holds and needs. No clock: it waits for everyone, or the host moves on.
  2. The walk: 20 s. Where a buyer and a seller meet, units change hands, and the clock runs at 25 % speed meanwhile.
  3. Close: 2.5 s in which each phone shows its receipt for the good.

Every sale pays the sales tax into the colony fund.

The month end In the game

The month end (the game calls it upkeep) settles the month in this order:

  1. Any month-end events strike.
  2. Players eat and drink: each player needs 2 Food and 1 Water for themselves, plus 1 Food for every 4 crew or part of 4, and 1 Water for every 6 crew or part of 6. A plot with a MULE making a raw good has a crew of 1, a factory 2, a green belt none. Energy for the plots was already paid in production.
  3. Colonists buy 1 Food and 1 Water for every 20 colonists from the store, at its selling price, as far as their wages reach. Fed, the colony grows by 4 %; short, it shrinks by 5 %.
  4. Goods spoil, and anything above 10 units of a good in a player's home depot is lost. The store's goods spoil at a quarter of the normal rate.
  5. The export ship, in its month, takes the store's surplus and leaves.
  6. The store builds MULEs from Smithore, until its corral holds 2 for each seat plus 2 spare.
  7. The colony (with the council on): law taxes are collected, the Federation pays 150 cr into the colony fund, the Governor is paid 50 cr from the fund, buildings pay their upkeep and go up, the building being funded takes what the fund can spare, and the colony's mood moves toward its target by at most 5 points.

Then the screens show the league table by Worth, with each player's change since last month, and how the colony is doing. The month end has no clock: it waits for every person to tap "On to month N" (computer players are on at once), or for the host's Continue. A dropped phone is waited for here too. After the last month's end the game goes straight to the final table.

What is changing

In the colony branch, not yet on trunk: at the month end the TV's Happiness gauge will open on last month's value, count to this month's, and pulse when it crosses into another mood band (PR #365, the Events team). A general strike will show as a badge on the TV, with the table waiting for the Governor's choice and strikers standing on the struck plots (PR #368, the Events team). See The table and the phones.

Events hold the clocks

When an event strikes at the start of development or the goods auction, that phase's clocks, the computer players' included, wait until the event's show has played: 7 s for a headline, 5.2 s for a spotlight, one shared 4 s roll call for all small bulletins, after a 1.8 s intro, and never more than 30 s in all. The colony's score

With the council on, the colony gets a score of its own at the end, beside the players' Worth and never added to it: (the colony's wealth + 100 cr for each point the air is cleaner than 100 + 1,000 cr for each point of prestige) × a happiness factor that runs from 0.8 at happiness 0 to 1.2 at happiness 100. The colony's wealth is the fund plus every player's Worth before the office line and any Collapse cut. The colony's wealth counts each player's cash, goods, land and MULEs, not their office or donations lines. Each player's donations are shown on the plaques, and count in their own Worth.

Example

The colony ends with 18,000 cr of wealth, air at 25 and 5 points of prestige, at happiness 70. Environment: 100 × (100 − 25) = 7,500. Prestige: 5 × 1,000 = 5,000. Factor: 0.8 + 0.4 × 0.70 = 1.08. Score: (18,000 + 7,500 + 5,000) × 1.08 = 32,940.

How long a game lasts and who wins In the game

A game lasts 12 months. It ends earlier when the land runs out: once the last free plot is claimed, the game goes on for 3 more months. The check is made at each month end, so the game ends at the month end of whichever comes first.

Example

The last free plot is claimed in month 6's land grant. The game ends at the end of month 9 (6 + 3), three months short of the full 12.

The winner is the player with the highest Worth after the last month end. On the screens, players level at the top share the win ("You share the win!"). The closing news line says the same: "Game over. Lena wins the colony!", or on a tie every tied player in seat order, "Game over. Lena and Brix share the win!". The final screens show the winner, the final table by Worth and, with the council on, the colony's score and plaques. Your phone shows your place, your Worth line by line, and the winner.

Colony collapse In the game

The colony's air runs from 0 (clean) to 100. Mines and smelters foul it; nature cleans 2 points a month for each player, and green belts and filters help. The air sits in one of four bands:

BandFromWhat it does
Clean0The export ship pays 10 % more
Smoggy30The dirty air can make a player sick in development; the colonists need 1.5 × the Medicine
Toxic60As smoggy, and every farm and well makes 1 less
Collapse80As toxic, and 5 % of colonists leave every month whatever they eat; every Worth is cut to 0.8

Collapse does not end the game. The game plays on to its normal end; if the air is in Collapse when the game ends, the Federation judges the colony a failure and every player's final Worth is cut by a fifth. While the air is in Collapse, the month-end screens show the cut already, with a banner saying why every Worth is lower. The colony's own score is counted before any cut.

These belong to the server, not the lobby; the host cannot change them today:

  • The council (elections, laws, the colony fund, buildings, mood) is on in every game. Without it there are no offices, no moving tax and no colony score.
  • The game length is 12 months unless the server is started with another.
  • Steered walking in development is on for every game.
  • The computer players' skill level is Trader for every computer seat.
