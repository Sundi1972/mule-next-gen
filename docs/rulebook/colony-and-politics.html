Colony and politics

The colony is a second thing to play for. It has a fund, a mood, buildings the table pays for together, a Governor you elect and a council that votes laws. At game over it gets a score of its own. This chapter covers how each part works today and what is still being built.

Everything in this chapter happens only when the host plays with politics, which is on by default. With politics off there is no Governor, no council, no colony fund ledger, no mood and no colony score. The sales tax still goes into the fund, and the game plays exactly as it did before politics existed. Almost every number below is a placeholder that will be tuned near the end and then shown to Heiko (ruled 4 Nov).

Mood: happiness and its bands In the game

The colony starts every game at happiness 60 (Content). At each month end it moves toward a target set by how the month went, but by at most 5 points a month. One bad month dents the mood; a run of them sinks it. Events can also move it at once, by any amount, without that limit.

What sets the month's target

The target starts at 60 and each of these moves it. The result is kept between 0 and 100.

Example

The mood is 60. This month the store had too little Food and too little Water for the colonists (−16), the air is at 45 (15 points over, −6), the Governor set the tax at 7% (−4), the Hospital stands (prestige 3, +6), and the richest player is 2,500 cr ahead of the poorest (−5). The target is 60 − 16 − 6 − 4 + 6 − 5 = 35. The mood can move only 5 a month, so it ends the month at 55: Restless. If nothing changes, it keeps falling by 5 a month: 50, 45, 40, then 35, where it stops.

The mood already changes how computer players vote: in an Unhappy or Unrest colony they will not back the sitting Governor if anyone else runs (see Computer players in politics). Next it will change which events happen (see Social events) and how fast the colony grows (see Mood and migration).

Why it is like this

The mood is one gauge whose value shapes events, not a second event system (G-5, approved by Heiko 28 Oct). The monthly cap stops it flip-flopping. Welfare spending counts 0 until the budget lines exist (Heiko, G-5 card, 4 Nov: "5. ok"). The weights are WS1's placeholders, tuned at the end.

The colony fund and its ledger In the game

The colony fund is the colony's own money. It is separate from the store, which trades with its own capital. The fund never goes below 0 and never borrows: when it cannot pay a bill in full, it pays what it has. Every credit in or out is recorded under where it came from or what it paid for, and each month's report carries that ledger.

The month end, in order

  1. The land tax, then the pollution tax, from each player in seat order.
  2. The Federation's grant of 200 cr (Decided by Heiko on 7 Nov).
  3. The Governor's pay.
  4. The colony's buildings: upkeep is paid, buildings under construction tick on, and the building being funded gets money (see Commissioning).
  5. The mood moves toward its target, and the gauges' month-end snapshot is taken.

The reserve

When the fund pays toward a building, it keeps 100 cr back, so next month's office pay is there. The reserve does not hold back a repair or strike pay: the Governor may empty the fund for those.

Why it is like this

Heiko ruled the income mix on 4 Nov ("3. all three"): the three law taxes, donations and the Federation's grant. One place in the engine writes to the fund, so the ledger can never miss a payment.

Commissioning, funding and building In the game

Only the Governor commissions, in planning, once any vote that month is over. The colony works on up to 3 buildings at once (Decided by Heiko on 6 Nov, pace set C): while three are being paid for or built, the Governor cannot start another. Each building can be built once per game, and there is no cancel. The phone's catalogue dims a building the Governor cannot start and says why, in the engine's words, for example "The colony already has 3 buildings under way".

From the next month end on, the fund pays toward the commissioned building. With more than one being paid for, it pays every open building in the order they were commissioned: a building whose credits are all in takes no more (it may still buy goods), so one waiting for its plot or goods never holds up the ones behind it, and the reserve is kept across all of them. For each, in turn:

  1. It pays as many of the missing credits as it can, keeping its 100 cr reserve.
  2. For each missing good, it buys what it can from the store at the store's selling price: no more than is missing, no more than the store will sell, and no more than the fund can pay above its reserve. No sales tax is charged on these.
  3. Once every credit and every good is in, building starts. No building waits for land. It does not count down until the next month end.

While the building waits, the goods it still needs count in the store's monthly demand, from the month it is commissioned until the last unit is in. Their price rises while it waits, so players have a reason to make them and sell them to the store, where the fund buys them at that price at the month end. A good only the export ship wanted before, such as Crystite or Electronics, is then no longer held to its off-world price. Once the building has every good, or is building, it adds nothing to the demand.

Players can speed this up by donating credits or the goods it still needs (see Donations).

Example: the Observatory needs 2 Crystite

The Governor commissions the Observatory in month 4. At that month end, after the grant and the Governor's pay, the fund holds 900 cr. It pays the 350 cr at once and has 550 left, 450 above its reserve. The store sells Crystite at 115 cr and starts the game with none on its shelves, so the fund buys nothing. The Observatory waits, fully paid in credits, for its 2 Crystite. In month 5 a player who mines Crystite donates 2 units: the plaque gets their name with 200 cr (2 × the price the store posts that day, 100: a gift counts at the posted price, not the 115 the store sells at), and building starts. It stands after two more month ends, at the end of month 7, and pays 25 cr upkeep from the end of month 8.

Why it is like this

The colony was first limited to one project open at a time, to keep its money from being spread thin. Decided by Heiko on 6 Nov (pace set C): up to three at once, with half-cost buildings, a 250 cr grant (200 since 7 Nov, Heiko) and computer Governors who commission at 10% of the credits, so a colony actually builds within a game. The fund pays every open building in the order they were commissioned. The fund buying missing goods from the store was a question put to Heiko on 4 Nov; the engine has it on behind a switch (COLONY.fundBuysGoods), so that only donors' goods count once it is turned off.

Heiko ruled on 6 Nov that the colony imports nothing: "The colony needs to produce them." Instead, a waiting building's missing goods count in the store's demand (G-5 AM-17, amended; C49). Measured on 10 simulated games before it was built: buildings standing at game over rose from 2 to 6. Electronics is still the good most waiting buildings lack, because the computer players do not yet answer its high price by building an Electronics factory.

Donations and plaques In the game

Any player can donate in planning toward the building being funded: credits, or units of a good it still needs, or both. You cannot give more credits than it still needs, a good it does not need, more units than are missing, or more than you have. Each refusal says exactly why ("The Observatory still needs 2 crystite").

Every donation goes on the building's plaque under your name, valued at credits plus each good at the store's price on the day you give it. The same value counts as taxes you have paid, which can earn you votes (see Your votes). It also counts 1:1 in your Worth (see Worth and scoring), so giving credits or goods leaves your Worth as it was: a good you give counted in your Worth at that same price the moment before. At game over, each player's plaques are shown beside their Worth.

Example

Kai gives the Hospital 200 cr and 1 Electronics on a day the store posts Electronics at 200. His plaque reads 400, and 400 is added to his taxes paid. His Worth does not move: the 400 leaves his credits and goods and joins his Donations line. That alone takes him past 300, worth one extra vote.

Computer players donate too Being built

In planning, after its other moves, a computer player gives once toward the building being funded: a share of the credits it holds above the reserve it keeps for next month's Food, Water and Energy. A Trader gives 10%, a Cautious player 15%, an Aggressive one 5%; a Saboteur never gives. It never gives more than the building still needs, and a gift under 10 cr is not made. Leading or trailing on Worth changes nothing yet. Credits only, no goods. Every number is a placeholder until the sim tunes it.

Example: a Trader holding 1,143 cr with a 143 cr reserve gives the Hospital 100 cr, and its plaque shows it.

The colony's grounds In the game

Every building stands on the colony's own grounds, a square around the town hall. None of it is a player's plot, and the map and its land never change for a building.

  • The grounds are a square of 5 by 5 cells with the town hall on the middle one.
  • Each building takes a block of cells the size of the building: the Stadium and the Spaceport upgrade take 2 by 2, the Park, the Hospital and the Observatory one cell each.
  • When the Governor commissions a building, it takes the first free block nearest the town hall. Nearest means: first the block whose farthest cell is closest to the hall, then the one whose nearest cell is closest, then going round clockwise from the cell above the hall. Nobody picks the place and there is no dice roll.
  • A closed or damaged building keeps its block. The next building takes the first block still free.
  • With the five buildings in the game the grounds never fill, whatever order they are built in. If no block of the right size were free, the Governor's phone would refuse with "There is no room on the colony's grounds for the Stadium" (or whichever building it is).
  • No building waits for land and the colony never bids at the land auction: every building starts as soon as it is fully funded (see Commissioning).
  • No rule reads the place. It changes no prestige, no air and nothing next to it.

Example: the order a computer Governor builds in

A computer Governor builds the most prestige first. The Spaceport upgrade takes the 2 by 2 block that starts at the cell just above the hall and reaches up and to the right; the Stadium the 2 by 2 block that starts at the cell just right of the hall and reaches right and down; the Observatory the cell below the hall; the Hospital the cell below and to the left; the Park the cell to the hall's left.

Example: built once funded

The Governor commissions the Hospital first: it takes the cell above the town hall. Once it stands, the Governor commissions the Park, which takes the cell above and to the right. At the month end the fund pays the Park's 420 cr and buys its 2 Alloy, so the Park starts building at once, and it stands at the next month end. No plot on the map changes owner.

Offices and elections In the game

The colony elects a Governor in months 1, 4, 7 and 10. A term runs until the next election. The Governor is paid 50 cr a month from the fund (as far as it reaches), and every term served adds 1,200 cr to that player's Worth, about one good month's income.

Running for office

You can declare in any month's planning, not only in election months: "Throw your hat in the ring" on the phone. Running for Governor carries a tax promise, one of three:

  • Cap: the tax will be no higher than a percent you name (0 to 15).
  • Lower: the tax will be below where it is when you take office.
  • Hold: the tax will stay where it is when you take office.

You can change your promise or withdraw until the field closes. It closes as an election month opens (in month 1, whose planning is the first, it stays open through the announcement). Nobody may serve more than 2 Governor terms in a row; after that someone else must hold it before they can run again.

Election day

  1. The announcement. Every phone and the TV show who runs and on what promise. Each player taps Ready to vote.
  2. The secret ballot. If two or more people run, each player picks one or abstains, then taps Cast my vote. Nobody, not even the TV, sees whom you chose. There is no clock: it closes once everyone has voted, or when the host closes it for someone who is away.
  3. The reveal. On the TV and every phone at once: the office's title, the candidates, then one block per vote growing beside each name, then the winner and the congratulations. One office with two candidates and six votes takes about 13 seconds.

Votes are weighted (see Your votes). Weights are taken as the ballot closes. The Governor then sets the sales tax.

How the winner is found

SituationResult
Nobody runsThe richest player (by cash) is appointed. An appointed Governor made no promise.
One person runsUnopposed; no ballot.

A tieThe Land Baron's ballot decides, if the Baron voted for one of the tied; otherwise the tied candidate with the most plots; otherwise a fair draw.

The Sheriff and the Land Baron

Only the Governor is elected today. The Sheriff comes back when it has powers (see Sabotage and the Sheriff); its pay of 30 cr a month is in the config but nobody draws it. The Land Baron is not an office: it is a title, fixed at the start of each planning phase, for whoever owns strictly the most plots. On a tie for most, the current Baron keeps it if they are among the tied; otherwise nobody holds it. The Baron gets 1 extra vote and breaks tied elections. The Baron's 10% discount at the land auction and choosing which lot goes first (design 05) are not built.

Why it is like this

Heiko, 28 Oct: "I should be able to run for office anytime. Even in no election months ... time for diplomacy and to adjust my promise", and the ballot closes once everybody has voted, then a dramatic reveal, office by office. On the Sheriff, Heiko 28 Oct: "Governor only for now". The month-1 tie-break used to favour the host's seat; the PM approved the Baron-then-land-then-draw order on 28 Oct. Office credit of 1,200 a term was WS1's measurement of a good month's Worth gain, accepted the same day.

  2. Proposes a law. It looks at every law: each law not in force at its middle setting (export tax 20%, land tax 10, pollution tax 20, emission cap 50) and the repeal of each law in force. The general strike In the game

A general strike is a colony event announced in planning. The colonists who work one good stop: this month every working plot of that good makes only 20% of its output, unless the Governor pays the strikers 250 cr from the fund. Paid, the plots produce in full. Unpaid, production's card no longer offers the pay-off: it reads “The strikers held: every food plot makes 20% this month.”

  • The strike hits the good that the most working plots make (a tie is settled on the event's dice). With no working plots there is no strike.
  • Only the Governor can pay, in that month's planning, once the votes are over. The reserve does not hold back: paying may empty the fund. If the fund holds less than 250, the Governor cannot pay and the strike holds. With no Governor, nobody can pay.
  • Unpaid, production makes 20% (rounded down) of each struck plot's output, after every other effect on it.
  • Its chance rises with a bad mood: +2 points a month when Restless, +5 Unhappy, +10 in Unrest, +3 more when the store's Food costs more than 1.5 times its usual 30 cr. It never happens in a Thriving colony, and never in the same month as a festival.

The colony is Unhappy and Food sells at 55. The strike's chance this planning is 5 + 3 = 8 points. It fires on Food. Lena, the Governor, has 400 in the fund and pays: the fund drops to 150, the news says "Lena pays the food strikers 250 cr", and every Food plot produces in full. The colonists' meal In the game

Colonists have no money and no wages, and pay no prices. At the month end, after the players eat, they take their Food and Water from the store's shelf, free: 1 Food and 1 Water for every 20 colonists, and more Food in a festival month, as far as the shelf holds them. The store keeps their month back from the players at the auction, so their need comes out of the store first. A dear price never starves them while the shelf has stock; only an empty shelf does.

Fed in full, the colony grows by 4% a month; short of either, it shrinks by 5%. The month end says how much they got. When nothing was missing: "The store fed the colony's 400 colonists". When something was, the line names it and turns red: "The store fed the colony's 400 colonists 19 of 20 Food and all their Water".

Because colonists pay nothing, nothing caps them in the store's sums: their full need counts in the store's demand at any price, and the store buys Food and Water at its step's own buying price, with no ceiling from what colonists could once pay (see How the posted price is set).

Example

A colony of 400 needs 20 Food and 20 Water. Food is posted at 300; the shelf holds 19 Food and 30 Water. The colonists take 19 Food and 20 Water and pay nothing, so the store's money is unchanged. They went one Food short, so the colony shrinks 5% to 380, and the month end reads "The store fed the colony's 400 colonists 19 of 20 Food and all their Water": the line counts the colonists who ate, before the month changed their number. Before this change they paid the store's selling price out of wages of at most 150 a unit for Food, so at that price they would have bought less than half their Food.

Why it is like this

Heiko, 6 Nov: colonists never hold or pay money, and the store's money is unlimited already, so nothing replaces their payment. With wages, a colony went hungry beside a stocked shelf whenever the price passed their wage. Under the store's new price steps the severe Food selling price (150) equals their old Food wage (150), so any trade that pulled the price higher would have starved them. WS7 agreed to ship the meal before the hardship rule (plan decision P-1).

Hunger, hardship and health Designed

Decided by Heiko on 6 Nov (the colony economy explainer, all recommendations approved). None of this is in the game yet: it ships together with the new Food and Medicine supply, so players first meet it in a colony that can be fed. Every number is a starting value, tuned with the rest.

Colonists eat free

In the game for Food and Water: see The colonists' meal. Still designed: with colony health they take their Medicine from the shelf the same way, free, as far as it holds it.

How short they went

Each month counts the share of their need that was missing, 0 to 1. Food and Water count, and the worse of the two counts. Missing Medicine is not counted here: it reaches the mood only through sickness (see Colony health), so nothing is counted twice (Heiko, 6 Nov). Energy does not touch the colonists. A tenth short or less costs nothing. Past that, a curve makes the cost grow faster than the shortfall: a little short is a nudge, very short is a blow.

  • The blow: the month's shortfall costs the mood at once, outside the usual 5-point monthly drift, up to 8 points with nothing to eat.
  • The memory: each hungry month adds to a memory of hardship and each fed month halves it (at most 2). It keeps the mood aiming up to 12 points lower and makes the unrest-band events up to half again likelier.
  • The colony's size: fed or within the tenth, it grows 4% a month; short of more, it shrinks by a tenth of the share missing (2% at a fifth short, 10% with nothing). The mood adds its push on settlers on top (see Mood and migration).

Example

A colony of 400 at mood 60 needs 20 Food. Month 1 it gets 19: nothing happens and it grows to 416. Month 2 it gets 10: the mood drifts down 2 and takes a blow of 2, to 56, and the colony shrinks 5% to 395. Month 3 it is fed: the mood climbs to 59, still held a little low by the memory, and is back at 60 in month 4.

Colony health

A health gauge shows the share of colonists who are well, from 100 down to 60: at most 4 in 10 are ever sick. Each month a few fall sick anyway; more when they went hungry or thirsty, more when Medicine was missing, and more the more are already sick (contagion). About half the sick recover each month, fewer when Medicine is short. Recovery is always stronger than contagion, so a colony that is fed and treated settles at about 3 in 100 sick: sickness cannot run away. Without Medicine it is a different story: in 10 simulated games where the store was short of Medicine almost every month, the sick share sat at the ceiling of 4 in 10 and stayed there. That is why colony health switches on only together with the Medicine supply. Sick colonists need up to 40% more Medicine, cost the mood up to 4 points of its aim and slow the colony's growth by up to 2% a month. How sickness spreads to players and crews is in the store and production chapters (WS5).

Electronics, a comfort

When the store can give the colonists their Electronics (1 per 50 colonists a month), the mood aims up to 4 points higher. Missing them never costs anything.

Why it is like this

Heiko: "falling short of 1 food doesn't immediately make all colonists unhappy… the worse the resource crisis the more likely they get unhappy and the more unhappy they get." Measured on 10 simulated games: today's rule costs the same for one unit missing as for a famine; a first, harsher set of numbers and a first sickness model with contagion as strong as recovery both spiralled into Unrest, and these softer numbers did not.

Reputation and deal cards Designed, not in the game yet, WS7

Every player has a reputation from 0 to 100, starting at 50, and it already counts in vote weight (+1 at 70 or more, −1 below 30). Today only the social event "blamed for the unrest" moves it, by 1 point (see Social events).

Decided by Heiko on 7 Nov (all of politics in the first release candidate, 18 Nov). How it will work:

  • Two kinds of card. In planning, a player opens a card from a whisper or from another player's name: a pact (no sabotage against each other for 1, 3 or 6 months), or a vote promise ("I vote for Brix for Governor" at the next election, or "I vote yes" or "no" on the next law). A card is always its sender's own promise. Nothing of value ever changes hands on a card.
  • One tap to answer. The other player accepts or declines; the sender can withdraw it until then. A card nobody answers expires when planning ends. An accepted vote promise also expires, unbroken, when its ballot is never held (an office with fewer than two candidates, a law ballot that never comes) or was already over, and every card still running expires when the game ends.
  • Kept and broken. A kept vote promise adds +3 to the player who kept it. A pact that runs its months kept adds +3 to both, but only in a game where sabotage is possible, so no pact can be farmed. Nobody gains from the same partner more than once a term. A broken vote promise costs −10; a broken pact (only a sabotage against the partner whose culprit becomes known breaks one, even if it comes out months later) costs −20. Its victim gains +3 (within the same once-a-term limit), unless the pact had already ended kept and paid them. Not voting on a promised ballot counts as breaking it; on the phone the promised choice is already picked, and Cast my vote sends it.
  • Never below one vote. Reputation below 30 still costs a vote, but nobody's vote weight falls below 1.
  • Everyone sees the breach. A broken card is public, in full: "Lena broke her promise to Brix: to vote for Brix". It never says what she voted instead. Active cards stay private to the two players.
  • A reputation chip beside every name, on the phones and the TV. Tapping it lists the public reasons it moved.
  • Computer players offer, answer, keep and sometimes break cards, without an API key. They refuse cards from anyone below 30, and accept the rest by what the card is worth to them (a cautious player wants more from it than a trader). Each offers at most 2 cards a month, in the announcement once it has decided whether to run and again in planning: its vote to a candidate it would vote for anyway, a yes or no on the law to whoever it costs most, or a pact once sabotage is in the game. A promised vote is kept unless breaking it gains more than its honesty times the reputation it would cost; a cautious player almost never breaks, a saboteur most easily. A candidate always votes for itself, so a computer player never promises its vote on an office it runs for, and the Governor always votes yes on the law.

Why it is like this

Heiko, 7 Nov: the release candidate carries all of politics, "with computer players that use them". A broken card shows what was promised, never the vote itself: the promise was signed openly; the ballot stays secret. Campaign law promises ("I won't propose a land tax") come after the release candidate (Heiko, 7 Nov, D-S3-1).

Sabotage and the Sheriff Designed, not in the game yet, WS7

There is no sabotage and no Sheriff in the game today: only the Governor is elected. Decided by Heiko on 7 Nov, for the first release candidate:

  • Secret orders. In planning, a player may place one sabotage order a month, in secret: a power cut on another player's working plot (that plot makes nothing that month), ordered from the plot's card, or a market rumour on a good (its store price opens 25% higher or lower at the auction), started from the good's line at the market. The card shows the cost and the chances it works and that it gets caught. The crew is paid at the month end.
  • Caught. A caught saboteur's name is on every screen ("Caught!"; or later, if the Sheriff's investigation finds them), and their reputation falls by 10; 20 more if they had a pact with the victim.
  • The Sheriff is elected again beside the Governor and gets a desk like the Governor's: 2 guards to post on players' plots each month (a power cut on a guarded plot always fails and is caught), one investigation a month into an unsolved sabotage (it finds the culprit half the time; a cold case stays cold), and fines of 50 to 300 cr in steps of 50 on a revealed culprit: half to the victim, half to the colony fund.
  • The sabotage ban becomes the fifth law: at ×2 or ×3 it doubles or triples the fines and the reputation a caught saboteur loses.
  • Computer players sabotage, run for Sheriff and do the Sheriff's job, without an API key.

Guards are secret: a guard shows only by the saboteur it catches. A failed order nobody saw stays the saboteur's own secret. Sabotage always says "Sabotage" and "someone" or a name; the colony's own trouble (strikes, walkouts, MULE thefts) never names a person.

Why it is like this

Heiko, 28 Oct: "Governor only for now", until the Sheriff has a job; slice 4 gives it one. Heiko, 7 Nov: an investigation finds the culprit half the time, so the doubt survives (D-S4-1); sabotage comes with politics and has no separate switch in the release candidate (D-S4-2).

The budget, the overrule and the inquiry Designed, not in the game yet, WS7

Today the fund pays every commissioned building all it holds above its reserve, and no player can challenge the Governor's tax. Decided by Heiko on 7 Nov; the release candidate carries the first part:

  • The budget. In the Governor's desk, once the month's votes are over, the Governor splits the fund above the reserve, in steps of 5%: Projects (the buildings; 100% until a Governor sets a budget) and Welfare (credits to the player last on Worth, at most 20% and 200 cr a month). What is not shared out stays in the fund, and so does any part of the Projects share the open buildings don't need: the share is "up to", never a payment. Every screen shows the split.
  • The overrule. Any player but the Governor can call an overrule vote on the Governor's tax from the tax line, once it differs from the start of the month; one call stands a month, and its caller can withdraw it before planning ends, which frees the call for someone else. As planning ends, everyone votes on every phone. More than half of all vote weight puts the tax back where it stood at the start of the month, before anything is sold. The Governor's promise is then checked against the tax put back.

After the release candidate: a Security line that pays the Sheriff's guards (at most 30% and 300 cr a month), the overrule on the budget, a snap election after a second overrule in a term, and the Sheriff's inquiry into the Governor's public breaches (a broken tax promise, broken cards, overrules passed), which can lead to a vote of no confidence.

Why it is like this

Heiko, 7 Nov (plan A): the release candidate carries the overrule on the tax with the Projects and Welfare lines; the rest follows right after. Measured on 40 simulated games before the design: about 6,000 cr a game sat idle in the fund. Heiko, 7 Nov: the overrule is held as planning ends (D-S5-2); a second overrule calls a snap election (D-S5-3); the inquiry looks at the Governor's public breaches (D-S5-4).
