Colony and politics

The colony is a second thing to play for. It has a fund, a mood, buildings the table pays for together, a Governor you elect and a council that votes laws. At game over it gets a score of its own. This chapter covers how each part works today and what is still being built.

Everything in this chapter happens only when the host plays with politics, which is on by default. With politics off there is no Governor, no council, no colony fund ledger, no mood and no colony score. The sales tax still goes into the fund, and the game plays exactly as it did before politics existed. Almost every number below is a placeholder that will be tuned near the end and then shown to Heiko (ruled 4 Nov).

Mood: happiness and its bands In the game

The colony starts every game at happiness 60 (Content). At each month end it moves toward a target set by how the month went, but by at most 5 points a month. One bad month dents the mood; a run of them sinks it. Events can also move it at once, by any amount, without that limit.

The bands

Thriving80 to 100
Content60 to 79
Restless40 to 59
Unhappy20 to 39
Unrest0 to 19

What sets the month's target

The target starts at 60 and each of these moves it. The result is kept between 0 and 100.

InputEffect on the target
Goods the store ran short of this month−8 for each good
Dirty air−0.4 for each air point above 30
A high sales tax−2 for each percent above 5%
The Governor broke their tax promise this month−10
Prestige standing+2 for each point, counting at most 15 points (so at most +30)
The gap between the richest and the poorest player, by cash plus land (300 cr a plot)−2 for each 1,000 cr of gap, counting at most 10 (so at most −20)

Example

The mood is 60. This month the store ran out of Food and Energy (−16), the air is at 45 (15 points over, −6), the Governor set the tax at 7% (−4), the Hospital stands (prestige 3, +6), and the richest player is 2,500 cr ahead of the poorest (−5). The target is 60 − 16 − 6 − 4 + 6 − 5 = 35. The mood can move only 5 a month, so it ends the month at 55: Restless. If nothing changes, it keeps falling by 5 a month: 50, 45, 40, then 35, where it stops.

The mood already changes how computer players vote: in an Unhappy or Unrest colony they will not back the sitting Governor if anyone else runs (see Computer players in politics). Next it will change which events happen (see Social events) and how fast the colony grows (see Mood and migration).

Why it is like this

The mood is one gauge whose value shapes events, not a second event system (G-5, approved by Heiko 28 Oct). The monthly cap stops it flip-flopping. Welfare spending counts 0 until the budget lines exist (Heiko, G-5 card, 4 Nov: "5. ok"). The weights are WS1's placeholders, tuned at the end.

The colony fund and its ledger In the game

The colony fund is the colony's own money. It is separate from the store, which trades with its own capital. The fund never goes below 0 and never borrows: when it cannot pay a bill in full, it pays what it has. Every credit in or out is recorded under where it came from or what it paid for, and each month's report carries that ledger.

Money inHow much
Sales taxOn every sale a player makes, at the rate the Governor sets (it starts at 5%). The seller pays it out of the sale.
The Federation's grant150 cr every month end. Flat for now; a rule that scales it is part of the final tuning.
Law taxesExport tax, land tax and pollution tax, when those laws are in force (see The council).
DonationsWhat players give toward a building (see Donations).
The Federation tax eventWhen it fires, 10% of each player's cash.
Money outHow much

UpkeepEach standing building's monthly upkeep.
RepairsMending a damaged building.
Strike payPaying off a general strike, 250 cr.
Site bidsA plot the colony wins at the land auction for a building.

The month end, in order

  1. The land tax, then the pollution tax, from each player in seat order.
  2. The Federation's grant of 150 cr.
  3. The Governor's pay.
  4. The colony's buildings: upkeep is paid, buildings under construction tick on, and the building being funded gets money (see Commissioning).
  5. The mood moves toward its target, and the gauges' month-end snapshot is taken.

The reserve

When the fund pays toward a building or bids for a site, it keeps 100 cr back, so next month's office pay is there. The reserve does not hold back a repair or strike pay: the Governor may empty the fund for those.

Why it is like this

Heiko ruled the income mix on 4 Nov ("3. all three"): the three law taxes, donations and the Federation's grant. One place in the engine writes to the fund, so the ledger can never miss a payment.

Commissioning, funding and building In the game

Only the Governor commissions, in planning, once any vote that month is over. The colony works on one building at a time: while one is being paid for or built, the Governor cannot start another. Each building can be built once per game, and there is no cancel. The phone's catalogue dims a building the Governor cannot start and says why, in the engine's words, for example "The colony is already building the Hospital".

From the next month end on, the fund pays toward the commissioned building:

  1. It pays as many of the missing credits as it can, keeping its 100 cr reserve.
  2. For each missing good, it buys what it can from the store at the store's selling price: no more than is missing, no more than the store will sell, and no more than the fund can pay above its reserve. No sales tax is charged on these.
  3. Once every credit and every good is in, and the building has its plot if it needs one, building starts. It does not count down until the next month end.

Players can speed this up by donating credits or the goods it still needs (see Donations).

Example: the Observatory needs 3 Crystite

The Governor commissions the Observatory in month 4. At that month end, after the grant and the Governor's pay, the fund holds 900 cr. It pays the 700 cr at once and has 200 left, so only 100 above its reserve. The store sells Crystite at about 110 cr and starts the game with none on its shelves, so the fund buys nothing. The Observatory waits, fully paid in credits, for its 3 Crystite. In month 5 a player who mines Crystite donates 3 units: the plaque gets their name with 300 cr (3 × the price the store posts that day, 100: a gift counts at the posted price, not the 110 the store sells at), and building starts. It stands after two more month ends, at the end of month 7, and pays 50 cr upkeep from the end of month 8.

Why it is like this

"One project open at a time" keeps the colony's money from being spread thin. The fund buying missing goods from the store was a question put to Heiko on 4 Nov; the engine has it on behind a switch (COLONY.fundBuysGoods), so that only donors' goods count once it is turned off.

What is changing

Whether the fund should buy goods from the store at all is still listed as open with Heiko in the PM's log of 4 Nov (the switch is on today). Owner: WS7, decision Heiko.

Donations and plaques In the game

Any player can donate in planning toward the building being funded: credits, or units of a good it still needs, or both. You cannot give more credits than it still needs, a good it does not need, more units than are missing, or more than you have. Each refusal says exactly why ("The Observatory still needs 2 crystite").

Every donation goes on the building's plaque under your name, valued at credits plus each good at the store's price on the day you give it. The same value counts as taxes you have paid, which can earn you votes (see Your votes). It also counts 1:1 in your Worth (see Worth and scoring), so giving credits or goods leaves your Worth as it was: a good you give counted in your Worth at that same price the moment before. At game over, each player's plaques are shown beside their Worth.

Example

Kai gives the Hospital 200 cr and 1 Electronics on a day the store posts Electronics at 200. His plaque reads 400, and 400 is added to his taxes paid. His Worth does not move: the 400 leaves his credits and goods and joins his Donations line. That alone takes him past 300, worth one extra vote.

What is changing

Computer players do not donate yet. WS9 has an approved plan for it (C28): placeholders per persona in the politics config, a decision of how much to give, and a test. Status: designed, not on any branch I could find.

Decided by Heiko on 5 Nov: donations count 1:1 in Worth, and the game now counts them (WS7's rule; built by the Auctions team, WS3, in the score). Your phone's line-by-line Worth does not show a Donations line yet: the Table team (WS4) adds it.

Decided by the PM on 6 Nov (G-5 AM-13): a gifted good counts at the store's price on the day, not its reference price, so a gift of goods is Worth-neutral too (WS7's rule and build).

The colony's site and its bid In the game

The Park, the Stadium and the Spaceport upgrade need a plot. The colony gets one at the land auction, bidding from the fund like a player, up to a top bid the Governor sets on the phone (in steps of 25 cr). A newly commissioned building has no bid, so the colony does not bid until the Governor sets one; everyone's phone shows "Waits for a plot: the Governor sets the colony's bid" until then.

The colony bids as a proxy, the way an auction site bids for you: on any player's plot that comes up, it leads at one credit over the best player's bid (or at the lot's minimum if nobody bids), as long as that stays under its cap. Its cap is the Governor's top bid or what the fund holds above its 100 cr reserve, whichever is lower. A player who bids up to the cap takes the lead. It never bids for MULEs or for the colony's own lots.

If the colony wins, the fund pays the seller (who pays sales tax as on any sale), any MULE on the plot goes back to the corral, and the plot becomes the colony's, bare, as the building's site. A plot with a building on it produces nothing.

Example

The Park waits for a plot. The Governor sets the bid to 300 cr; the fund holds 350, so the cap is 250 (350 minus the 100 reserve). Brix lists a plot with a minimum of 120. Lena bids 180: the colony leads at 181. Lena walks to 250, the cap: now she leads, and the colony stops. Had she stopped at 200, the colony would have won at 201.

Why it is like this

Heiko, G-5 card, 4 Nov: "4. it bids". The proxy rule was approved by the PM on 5 Nov (G-5 AM-8).

Offices and elections In the game

The colony elects a Governor in months 1, 4, 7 and 10. A term runs until the next election. The Governor is paid 50 cr a month from the fund (as far as it reaches), and every term served adds 1,200 cr to that player's Worth, about one good month's income.

Running for office

You can declare in any month's planning, not only in election months: "Throw your hat in the ring" on the phone. Running for Governor carries a tax promise, one of three:

  • Cap: the tax will be no higher than a percent you name (0 to 15).
  • Lower: the tax will be below where it is when you take office.
  • Hold: the tax will stay where it is when you take office.

You can change your promise or withdraw until the field closes. It closes as an election month opens (in month 1, whose planning is the first, it stays open through the announcement). Nobody may serve more than 2 Governor terms in a row; after that someone else must hold it before they can run again.

Election day

  1. The announcement. Every phone and the TV show who runs and on what promise. Each player taps Ready to vote.
  2. The secret ballot. If two or more people run, each player picks one or abstains, then taps Cast my vote. Nobody, not even the TV, sees whom you chose. There is no clock: it closes once everyone has voted, or when the host closes it for someone who is away.
  3. The reveal. On the TV and every phone at once: the office's title, the candidates, then one block per vote growing beside each name, then the winner and the congratulations. One office with two candidates and six votes takes about 13 seconds.

Votes are weighted (see Your votes). Weights are taken as the ballot closes. The Governor then sets the sales tax.

How the winner is found

SituationResult
Nobody runsThe richest player (by cash) is appointed. An appointed Governor made no promise.
One person runsUnopposed; no ballot.
Most weighted votesWins.
A tieThe Land Baron's ballot decides, if the Baron voted for one of the tied; otherwise the tied candidate with the most plots; otherwise a fair draw.

The Sheriff and the Land Baron

Only the Governor is elected today. The Sheriff comes back when it has powers (see Sabotage and the Sheriff); its pay of 30 cr a month is in the config but nobody draws it. The Land Baron is not an office: it is a title, fixed at the start of each planning phase, for whoever owns strictly the most plots. On a tie for most, the current Baron keeps it if they are among the tied; otherwise nobody holds it. The Baron gets 1 extra vote and breaks tied elections. The Baron's 10% discount at the land auction and choosing which lot goes first (design 05) are not built.

Why it is like this

Heiko, 28 Oct: "I should be able to run for office anytime. Even in no election months ... time for diplomacy and to adjust my promise", and the ballot closes once everybody has voted, then a dramatic reveal, office by office. On the Sheriff, Heiko 28 Oct: "Governor only for now". The month-1 tie-break used to favour the host's seat; the PM approved the Baron-then-land-then-draw order on 28 Oct. Office credit of 1,200 a term was WS1's measurement of a good month's Worth gain, accepted the same day.

Your votes: vote weight In the game

Everyone has 1 vote, plus extras worked out fresh every time a ballot closes. The same weights count in elections and law votes. The TV's election screen shows everyone's votes, and your phone shows your own on each ballot.

The Governor's sales tax and promises In the game

The sales tax starts at 5%. Once the month's votes are over, the Governor can set it on the Governor desk, in whole percent from 0 to 15, and at most 5 points up or down from where it stood when the month began. The desk shows the allowed range and the promise the Governor ran on.

At the end of each planning phase the game checks the Governor's promise against the tax as it stands. A broken promise lowers that month's mood target by 10. Nothing is checked in a planning phase whose election was still open, since the Governor could not set the tax yet.

The council and its four laws In the game

Once a month, after the votes are over, the Governor may propose one law, a new setting for a law in force, or the repeal of one. The proposal is voted on at the start of the next month's planning, after that month's election if there is one. If the proposer is no longer Governor by then, the proposal is dropped.

LawSettingsWhat it doesWhen it is charged
Export tax10, 20 or 30%That share of each unit a player sells to the export ship goes into the fund, after the sales tax and out of the sale. Sales to the store or to another player pay none.At each sale to the ship
Land tax5, 10 or 20 cr a plotEvery owner pays per plot owned.Month end
Pollution tax10, 20 or 40 cr a pointEach player pays per point of colony pollution their plots caused that month.Month end
Emission capAir level 40, 50 or 60No MULE may be outfitted for Rare earth or Alloy (no new Rare earth mines or smelters) while the air is above the level. The refusal comes before you pay.When outfitting

A player who cannot pay a land or pollution tax pays what they have; the rest is waived, not owed. Every law tax counts toward that player's taxes paid, and so toward votes.

Rules for laws

  • At most 3 laws are in force at once. A fourth must name the law it replaces.
  • A proposal that fails cannot be put again for 2 months: one that fails in month 3 can come back in month 6.
  • A law stays in force until it is replaced or repealed. A repeal is a normal proposal.
  • The sales tax is not a law: the Governor sets it alone.

Example

A land tax of 10 is in force. At month end Ossa owns 7 plots and has 500 cr: she pays 70. If she had only 40 she would pay 40 and the other 30 would be waived. With an export tax of 20% and the sales tax at 5%, a unit sold to the ship for 100 leaves the seller 75.

Why it is like this

Heiko, G-5 card, 4 Nov: "2. thous four now": the first laws are the land tax, export tax, pollution tax and emission cap, the four that only move money or block an action. The sabotage ban and the Sheriff's fines come with the Sheriff. The other laws in design 05 (price cap, strategic reserve, anti-hoarding, windfall tax, anti-monopoly, eminent domain, green zone) wait for the systems they need. Slice 2a and the colony joined the MVP the same day ("7. yes i want them in").

The law ballot on the phones and the TV council In the game

All month, every phone except the Governor's carries the pending proposal under This month as "Next month's vote", with what it would cost that player ("For you"). When the ballot opens it takes the stage on every phone and on the TV.

  • On your phone: the proposal, what it costs you a month (or saves you, for a repeal), Yes or No, then Done. You can change your vote until the last vote is in. There is no clock; the host can close the vote for someone who is away, and the votes as they stand are counted.
  • On the TV: "Vote on your phone. Nobody sees how." The Governor's proposal in large type, what it means, each player's cost, and who has voted, never how.
  • The result: after everyone is Done, the weighted Yes and No totals and Passed or Failed show on every screen at once for 4 seconds. A passed law takes effect when that ends. The laws in force then sit as chips along the TV's foot and on the Council desk ("Land tax 10 cr · since month 3").

The Governor's own ballot starts at Yes, and the Governor's ballot breaks a tie: a tie passes if the Governor voted Yes.

How much each vote weighs In the game

Each vote is weighed when the ballot closes, from what its voter holds at that moment: land, good name (reputation), taxes paid and the Land Baron's extra vote (see Your votes). A player who bought land since the election may carry more votes than at the election. Your phone's "Your vote weighs N" already reads this live weight.

The TV's line under the vote count says the same: "Each vote weighs by what its voter holds when the ballot closes: land, good name, taxes paid, the Land Baron."

Why it is like this

The engine's count (tally, RULE-42) asks for each voter's weight as the ballot closes, and the weight is always worked out from the game as it stands (RULE-4). The rulebook writers found the TV's old words ("as it did at the election") disagreed, and PR #392 corrected them. WS7, which owns the rule, has been asked to confirm the new wording.

What is changing

Nothing pending. The TV line (PR #392, Events team, WS8) reached trunk with the colony batch (#413) on 5 Nov.

Computer players in politics In the game

Every computer seat, Classic or Smart, plays politics with the same built-in rules; Claude does not take politics decisions yet. What a computer player does depends on its persona: Trader, Cautious, Aggressive or Saboteur. On election day it waits a human's pause (0.8 to 2.5 seconds) before it acts and presses Done; on a law ballot it votes and presses Done at once, so no ballot waits for a computer seat.

  • When it runs: it decides once per election, in the planning phase one month before (for month 1, at month 1's announcement). Its promise follows its tax target: a cap at its target if that is above today's tax, "lower" if below, "hold" if equal. (There are chances for running for Sheriff too, but no Sheriff is elected.)
  • How it votes for Governor: for itself if it runs; otherwise for the candidate with the fewest plots, so the table leans against the leader (a tie is a fair draw; a Rookie picks at random). In an Unhappy or Unrest colony it will not vote for the sitting Governor if anyone else runs.
  • How it votes on a law: the Governor always votes Yes. The Land Baron always votes No to a land tax. A Rookie flips a coin. Otherwise it votes Yes to a new law if the law costs it no more than the middle player's cost, and Yes to a repeal if the law costs it more than the middle player.

What a computer Governor does

Each planning phase, once the votes are over, a computer Governor acts in this order:

  1. Sets the tax. One step toward its persona's target, at most 5 points, plus 2 points when the fund is low (from month 2, under three months of the Governor's pay, so under 150 cr), always inside the month's allowed range.
  2. Proposes a law. It looks at every law: each law not in force at its middle setting (export tax 20%, land tax 10, pollution tax 20, emission cap 50) and the repeal of each law in force. It picks the one that costs the leader (the other player with the highest Worth) most compared with itself. It proposes only if that gap is at least 20 cr a month, and then only with its persona's chance. With three laws in force, the new one replaces the law that costs it most.
  3. Pays a general strike if its persona does and the fund holds the 250 cr.
  4. Repairs damaged buildings, the most prestige first, while the fund covers the cost, if its persona does.
  5. Bids for sites: gives every building waiting for a plot its persona's top bid.
  6. Commissions the building with the most prestige whose credits the fund holds now (the cheaper one on a tie), if its persona commissions and the colony is free to start one. It picks a building that needs a plot only if its persona bids for sites, and sets that bid at once.

Example

An Aggressive computer is Governor in month 5. The tax stands at 5% and the fund at 1,100 cr. Its target is 8%, so it sets 8% (a step of 3). The fund is not low, so there is no bump. Nothing is damaged and there is no strike. The Spaceport upgrade's 1,380 cr is more than the fund holds; the Stadium's 1,000 is not, and it has the most prestige of the rest (4). It commissions the Stadium and bids up to 500 cr for its plot. Had a Saboteur been Governor, it would have raised the tax toward 10% and built nothing.

The council's own draws use a separate seeded stream per seat and month, so adding the council moved no other dice in a replay. All persona numbers are placeholders until the sim tunes them.

The general strike In the game

A general strike is a colony event announced in planning. The colonists who work one good stop: this month every working plot of that good makes only 20% of its output, unless the Governor pays the strikers 250 cr from the fund. Paid, the plots produce in full.

  • The strike hits the good that the most working plots make (a tie is settled on the event's dice). With no working plots there is no strike.
  • Only the Governor can pay, in that month's planning, once the votes are over. The reserve does not hold back: paying may empty the fund. If the fund holds less than 250, the Governor cannot pay and the strike holds. With no Governor, nobody can pay.
  • Unpaid, production makes 20% (rounded down) of each struck plot's output, after every other effect on it.
  • Its chance rises with a bad mood: +2 points a month when Restless, +5 Unhappy, +10 in Unrest, +3 more when the store's Food costs more than 1.5 times its usual 30 cr. It never happens in a Thriving colony, and never in the same month as a festival.

Example

The colony is Unhappy and Food sells at 55. The strike's chance this planning is 5 + 3 = 8 points. It fires on Food. Lena, the Governor, has 400 in the fund and pays: the fund drops to 150, the news says "Lena pays the food strikers 250 cr", and every Food plot produces in full. Had she not, a plot that would make 5 Food makes 1.

Why it is like this

The catalogue Heiko approved (rev 22) says a strike is announced now and this month every plot of one good makes 20% unless the Governor pays. The share has one number, COLONY.strike.output, beside the cost (PM ruling 5 Nov). On how it looks, the PM approved WS8's option A on 5 Nov: the Governor's choice sits in the phone's Now card, so the map stays in view and the decision cannot drift.

On screen: a STRIKE or PAID badge in the TV header, strikers on the struck good's plots in planning, the Governor's Now card with Pay and Let them strike once the votes are over (or a pay line under Now for a Governor already Ready), everyone else's alert line, "Waiting for Kai, the Governor" on the TV, and the paid news line. The colony score at game over In the game

At game over the colony gets its own score, beside the players' Worth. The winner is still the player with the highest Worth; the colony score says how the colony did. It is worked out from the final state alone, so a replay of the same seed and moves gives the same score.

TermHow it is counted
WealthThe Wealth gauge: the fund plus everything every player owns, in credits.
Environment100 cr for each point the air is cleaner than 100.
Prestige1,000 cr for each point of prestige standing.
Happiness factorFrom 0.8 at happiness 0 to 1.2 at happiness 100, in a straight line.

Colony score = (Wealth + Environment + Prestige) × the happiness factor, rounded once. Each player's standing at the end is their Worth, with the plaques they earned shown beside it, not added to it. The TV shows the colony score as a band under the winner screen, with the sum, and who gave most.

Example

The colony ends with Wealth 26,400, air at 34, the Park standing (prestige 2) and happiness 64. Environment is 100 × (100 − 34) = 6,600. Prestige is 1,000 × 2 = 2,000. The factor is 0.8 + 0.4 × 0.64 = 1.056. The score is (26,400 + 6,600 + 2,000) × 1.056 = 36,960. The same colony at happiness 0 would score 28,000; at 100, 42,000.

A colony whose air ends in Collapse still cuts every player's Worth by the Collapse rule (× 0.8); that cut does not touch the colony score's terms.

Why it is like this

Heiko's scoring model, 28 Oct: one Worth number decides the winner; the colony score is separate, "colony wealth + air + prestige, × mood (~0.8 unrest .. 1.2 thriving)". The PM approved the exact formula on 5 Nov (RULE-25). The rates are placeholders, tuned at the end.

Reputation and deal cards Backlog

Every player has a reputation from 0 to 100, starting at 50, and it already counts in vote weight (+1 at 70 or more, −1 below 30). On trunk nothing changes it yet, so it is 50 for everyone all game. In the colony branch, "blamed for the unrest" takes 1 point.

The design (politics slice 3) makes reputation move: a deal card attached to a whisper turns a promise into something one tap accepts ("a non-aggression pact", "my vote for your law"), and breaking a signed card costs reputation, so it costs votes next time. Deal cards carry pacts, votes and promises only; nothing of value ever changes hands outside an auction. Borrowed votes (another player's votes for one month, by deal card) are part of the same design. Computer players may bluff about their own intentions, never about facts (Heiko, 28 Oct).

Sabotage and the Sheriff Backlog

There is no sabotage order and no Sheriff in the game today. The design (politics slice 4): players order sabotage in secret; the elected Sheriff guards plots (a sabotage against a guarded plot fails and reveals the saboteur), investigates one past sabotage, theft or broken deal a month, fines a revealed culprit (half to the victim, half to the fund), inspects one player a month for a broken law, and can open an inquiry into the Governor. The sabotage ban law (fines doubled or tripled) comes with it. The Sheriff is paid 30 cr a month; nobody may hold Governor and Sheriff at once.

Computer personas already carry a spite weight and a per-level sabotage switch (off for Rookie), used today only to bid up the leader in the auction.

Why it is like this

Heiko, 28 Oct: "Governor only for now": the Sheriff is elected only once slice 4 gives it powers. Heiko, 4 Nov: the sabotage ban and the Sheriff's fines go with slice 4, outside the MVP.

The budget lines, overrule and inquiry Backlog

Today the Governor spends the fund only on buildings, repairs, site bids and strike pay. The design (politics slice 5) has the Governor split the fund each month across five budget lines (Reserve for emergency imports, Security for the Sheriff's guards, Environment for clean-up squads, Welfare for the last-placed player, Projects for building and upkeep), with a protective kind of colony project (reservoir, levee, meteor shield and others) on the same pipeline as the prestige objects. Any player could call an overrule vote against the Governor's tax or budget once a month; twice overruled in a term means a snap election. The Sheriff's inquiry could lead to a no-confidence vote.

Outside the MVP; no lane is on it. Welfare will then feed the mood (it counts 0 until then). Owner: WS7.
