The table and the phones

What the big screen shows in every phase, what each phone shows, how you join and get back in after a drop, how chat and the event shows reach everyone, and how the game plays on a phone alone.

Public on the TV, private on the phone In the game

The game is played with one shared big screen and a phone in every player's hand. The rule behind every screen: public things go on the big screen, private things stay on the phone. The TV never shows what a player could want to hide: the goods you hold, your ground surveys, your vote, your whispers, your Worth line by line. Money, plots and each player's Worth total are public, as in the original.

The big screen is optional. The game runs on the server; the TV and every phone are just screens connected to it. The TV has no seat and no controls, and it never mirrors a phone. Without a TV, each phone shows everything it needs, and the game plays the same (see A phone alone).

Any screen with a browser can be the TV: a laptop, a PC on an HDMI cable, a smart TV's browser. The TV page is drawn at 1280 × 720 and scaled to fit the screen, so it reads the same on any size. The phone page also works on a laptop: from a window 1100 px wide the same screens grow a wider side rail and larger type, and development can be steered with the keyboard.

Why it is like this

From the design doc on big-screen mode: "If a phone ever needs the big screen to make a private decision, or the big screen ever needs a phone to follow the game, the design is wrong."

Joining, the lobby and starting In the game

On a phone

Open the game's address. The first screen, "Pull up a chair", asks for your name and either a room code to join or Start a new game. It also says: "Got a TV or laptop? Open /table on it for the big screen." Whoever starts a new game gets a four-letter room code (no vowels, so codes never spell words) and becomes the host.

In the lobby, step 1 of 2 is "Make your colonist": pick one of the six species and one of the six colours. You are drawn large on the stage, in your colour, with your species' perk. The side rail is the table: "At the table · N of 6", every seat with its creature, name and what it is (a computer player, the host, or AWAY when its phone has gone). A colour a person has taken is theirs; a computer player's colour can be taken, and the computer player moves to another.

The host's rail also has Add AI, a ✕ to remove a seat, Close the table and Start. Removing a seat or closing the table asks for one confirming tap first. Start needs at least 2 seats. Anyone else can leave the table before the game starts.

On the TV

Open /table on the big screen. It asks for the room code shown on the host's phone ("Show this table"); a link with the code in it opens the table straight away. The TV lobby shows a large QR code to scan, the join address, the room code in big letters, every seat with its creature and whether it is a computer player, the host, Ready or Away, and "Waiting for the host to start".

With a TV connected, the phone's QR card is small (the TV carries the big one). With no TV, the phone draws its QR big and puts it first, under the next-step card, because it is the only way in.

Why it is like this

Heiko ruled on 21 Oct to keep the compact lobby (version 1 of the approved mocks) and, when no TV is connected, to use the bigger room card with a larger QR in the same spot.

Where the screen shows a ready count, the gauges take a row of their own under the title. The table chat's ticker runs under the header once someone has spoken, except on the goods auction's walk (see The TV fits a real table).

Planning on the TV

The ready list shows each person's place by Worth ("1st", "2nd"), their creature and one word: PLANNING, READY, or AWAY · PLANNING for a dropped phone. Computer players, always ready, share one dimmed row. The map's caption reads the room code, the colonists, the MULEs in the corral and their price. The market tiles show six goods with their price and how many months of demand the store holds, amber under one month and red under half a month.

The goods auction on the TV

Each running deal is a row ("Lena buys from the store at 34", for example), newest first. The newest shows its speed: chevrons that fill as the pair stays matched, "faster and faster" until "full speed"; older deals take one line each. When no deal runs, a help line explains the board: "Sellers push the red line down, buyers push the green line up. When the two lines meet, the goods change hands." The TV plays the auction's sounds: a tick-tock clock that speeds up as time runs out and slows while trading, a click for every unit, a bell struck three times when a good's walk ends.

Events on the TV

When events strike, the TV plays their show over the phase screen: the header with a pip for each event, the living map where each event lands on its plots, a log of what happened, and a colony bar with every player's slot glowing while their spotlight plays and a "sick" tag once their sickness lands. Production plays its events inside its own show. See Event shows.

The TV fits a real table In the game

Played at a real table, some TV screens ran out of room. Every screen below is checked at 1920 × 1080: nothing runs off the bottom, and every row it promises is shown whole.

  • The goods auction's walk has no chat ticker at all. The speakers' bubbles on the board carry what is said, and the board stays one size as deals start and stop. The newest deal keeps its speed line; older deals take one line each, so two deals fit under the store's box. The help line shows only while no deal runs. The status stage, before the walk, keeps its three chat lines.
  • Planning and the month end: the ready count ("2 / 3 READY") sits beside the title, and the colony's four gauges move to a full-width row of their own under it, so the count never covers a gauge.
  • Titles stay on one line: "End of the month" no longer wraps, and a long title never runs under the buttons at the header's right.

The phone: always sideways In the game

The phone is played held sideways for the whole game. Held upright it shows only "turn your phone"; the game stays loaded underneath, so nothing is lost while you turn it. The screens are drawn for a phone of 844 × 390 and still fit a small one of 667 × 375.

Every phone screen has the same frame. The stage on the left holds the phase's content: the map, the board, the hall. The rail on the right holds, from the top:

  • the month ("Month 3 / 12") and the phase's name, with the clock when the phase has one, or a ready count when it has none;
  • your creature and name, the Day/Night switch, "Move to my phone", and your money; in planning, under your money, your place ("2nd of 4"), which opens the standings;
  • Now: the one next step and why;
  • the screen's own controls;
  • the table chat: everyone's seat and the last two lines;
  • at the bottom, within thumb reach, the main action.

Why it is like this

Heiko, 28 Oct: "phone is always landscape btw and the steering control should be laying over the card", then "Whole game" when asked whether that meant every screen. This replaced the design doc's earlier "Phones stay portrait".

Planning on the phone: the hub In the game

Planning opens the month's hub (the team calls its built parts "Today"). The stage holds:

  • The month's roadmap: Plan, Land grant, Land auction (only in a month with a lot), Develop, Produce, Goods auction, this step lit, and one plain line on what to do now and what comes next. With the council on, the offices sit under it.
  • What you need this month: Food, Energy and Water against what you hold, red where you are short. A tap opens the Colony desk.
  • This month: everything the month allows, each a door into a desk or a map tool. It heads with the export ship ("Export ship in month 6: it buys the colony's leftovers", or "in port"). Its items: Next month's vote, Throw your hat in the ring (before an election), Geo drone, Sell a plot or MULE, and the Governor's desk for the Governor.
  • The colony map with its tools: Look (tap a plot to see what it can make and whose it is), Geo drone (pick up to 2 free plots and send it) and Sell a plot (tap your plot or one with your MULE).
  • Under the map: the store's prices and stock in one row of goods.

The rail holds Now, the open tool's panel, and the desks: Colony (what you need, the news, the colony), Talk (the table chat, with an unread badge), Council (with the council on), Market (put a plot or MULE up; the month's line-up of lots) and, for the Governor, the Governor's desk (set the tax, propose a law). In an election month, election day takes the stage until the votes are over.

At the foot, "I'm ready". Once you are, it shows who the table still waits for; the host can then continue without them after one confirming tap ("End planning for everyone now? … stop where they are, and the land grant starts.").

Development on the sideways phone In the game

In development the phone becomes an arcade controller. The whole colony map fills the screen, with every colonist walking live. A translucent stick lies over the bottom-left corner and the action button over the bottom-right. When you walk into the store, the stage becomes the store's hall: six shopfronts for the raw goods along the top, the works for the factories, the corral and the pub.

  • The button says what it will do where you stand: BUY MULE, ANALYSE, FIT FILTER, TAKE MULE, CATCH MULE, FIGHT, GRAB THE GLINT, PUB · END TURN and so on. It is dim when there is nothing to do. The server decides; a press is a request.
  • A ring shows your next step: the store and its corral when you have no MULE, the outfitters you can pay when it is empty, then the best of your free plots for its good. When you cannot afford a MULE, it points to what you can do instead: analyse a plot, or the pub.
  • The side rooms beside the map hold the clock, your money and your pace line, for example "Your pace 47%: you ate 1 of the 3 Food you need", with a tap for what to do about it next month. On a small phone the status lies in a bar over the map's top edge.
  • When your turn ends, a card says "In the pub: your turn is over" or "Development over", with how many MULEs you installed and how long the others still walk.
  • If no update has arrived for 1 s, the phone shows "reconnecting" and stops guessing where you are.

On a laptop, the arrow keys steer and SPACE presses the button; you can also hold the mouse on a spot to walk there and click the lit thing to act.

Chat, whispers, emotes and bubbles In the game

You type in the Talk desk in planning: to the whole table, or whispered to one player ("WHISPER · ONLY YOU AND KAI SEE THIS"). Five quick emotes sit above the box: Deal?, Nice!, Traitor!, Ha! and Help!. A line holds up to 280 characters. A whisper reaches only its two ends, never the TV; a whisper between two people stays hidden from the computer players too.

Every screen, the TV included, shows what was said:

  • A speech bubble pops at the speaker's creature wherever the screen draws it (on the map, on the auction board, in the store's hall). Where the screen does not draw the speaker, the bubble opens at their place in the seat row. One bubble shows at a time on each screen, so they never cover each other: each shows for at least 2 s and at most 4.5 s, and a line that would wait more than 4 s for its turn gets no bubble. A new phase ends every bubble.
  • The ticker keeps the last lines, newest first: two on a phone (in the rail, under its content), three on the TV (under the header). The TV's goods-auction walk has none; its bubbles carry the chat. A whisper is marked 🔒.
  • Emotes float at the top of every phone's stage for 3.5 s.

Computer players talk too: each says at most 3 lines a month and one a phase, up to 140 characters, about what it just decided. A public line from a person reaches every computer player, a whisper only the one it was for.

Why it is like this

Heiko, 22 Oct: live table chat "Live on every screen", a speech bubble at the speaker and a short ticker on the phones and the TV in every phase. He approved the design the same day (ticker 2 lines on a phone, 3 on the TV; whispers only on the two screens involved, with a lock, never on the TV), with the PM's condition that bubbles never cover each other or the ticker.

What is changing

Only the planning hub has a place to type. In every other phase you can read the chat and see bubbles but not write, and no ruling says whether that is intended. Owner: the UX team (WS6).

A phone alone In the game

The game plays without a TV. A phone on its own (one person against computer players, or several people each on their own phone with no big screen) notices that no TV is connected and takes over what the TV would show:

  • The lobby draws the room's QR big, first on the screen.
  • Every phase already shows the whole map: the hub's colony map, the land grant's lit plot, the arcade's whole colony, the auction board.
  • Production and events play their full show on the phone, and a tap skips ahead, instead of the light card and the buzz.
  • The month end fits the league table and the colony panel on one stage.
  • Game over shows the colony's report under your result.

This follows the TV live: if the TV disconnects mid-game, the phones switch to these screens; when it comes back, they switch back.

Dropping out and getting back in In the game

  • A dropped connection: the phone shows "CONNECTION LOST · REJOINING…" and tries again by itself, waiting a little longer each time, up to 5 s between tries. It takes its seat back with the seat's own key.
  • A reload: the phone remembers its seat and takes it back by itself, unless you opened a link to another table.
  • A new device: "Move to my phone" (in every game screen's rail, and in the lobby) shows a QR and a one-time code. Opened on the other device within 2 minutes, it moves your seat there, with your money, goods and plots, and the old screen steps aside ("You're playing on another device now"). The game does not pause meanwhile.
  • Nobody new can join a game that has started. A seat whose device is lost and has no saved key cannot be taken back.
  • The host passes to the next connected person while the host's phone is gone, and back when it returns.
  • A room that nobody has been connected to for 30 minutes is closed.

While you are away, the table treats you by phase: planning, the votes, the goods auction's side pick and the month end wait for you (the host can go on without you); in the land grant you just do not claim; your colonist stands still in development; in the walks you stop where you stand; production does not wait for you. The TV and the other phones mark you AWAY.

Reduced motion and accessibility In the game

  • Reduced motion: on a device set to reduce motion, the event shows' looping effects hold still and their entrances become a plain fade; the store and the MULEs stop animating; creatures stop their walking cycle and hold a still lean; the show's vibrate icon stops shaking. The production show, the election reveal and the event shows all follow the setting live.
  • Telling players apart: every player has both a colour and a species shape, so colour is never the only clue. Changes in the league table carry an arrow and a sign (↑ +120, ↓ −40), never colour alone.
  • Day and Night: every screen has a Day/Night switch; the phone remembers its choice.
  • Screen readers: the chat ticker is announced as it changes; private lines carry an "only you see this" label; the creature in your header is named ("You, Bramblet").
  • Keyboard: on a laptop, development is played with the arrow keys and SPACE.
  • Readable from the couch: the TV is drawn for 3 metres: large type, few words.

What is changing

The design doc's host Pause button (for dinner and phone calls, shown on the TV) is not built: the game never pauses, and a move to another phone says so. Owner: the Table team (WS4).

The colony on the TV: mood drift and strikes Being built

Two colony shows are built in the colony branch and are not on trunk yet.

The month end's mood drift

At the month end, the TV's Happiness gauge opens on last month's value and, about 1.2 s later, counts to this month's. When the count crosses into another band (Restless into Unhappy, for example), the band's name flips and the gauge pulses. It shows no reasons, only "↓ n this month". It plays once, as the TV sees the month end arrive; a TV that reloads into the month end, or a device set to reduce motion, shows the end state at once. Nothing plays in month 1, with an unchanged mood, or with the council off.

Strikes

During a general strike, the TV header carries a strike badge ("STRIKE · Food 20%", then "PAID · −250 cr" once paid), the TV shows whose choice the strike waits on ("Waiting for Kai, the Governor"), and strikers with placards stand over every plot of the struck good on the map. The Governor's phone shows the strike in its Now card, with "Pay the strikers" and "Let them strike"; everyone else's phone shows a line saying the strike is on and who decides.
