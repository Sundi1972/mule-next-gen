The table and the phones

What the big screen shows in every phase, what each phone shows, how you join and get back in after a drop, how chat and the event shows reach everyone, and how the game plays on a phone alone.

Public on the TV, private on the phone In the game

The game is played with one shared big screen and a phone in every player's hand. The rule behind every screen: public things go on the big screen, private things stay on the phone. The TV never shows what a player could want to hide: the goods you hold, your ground surveys, your vote, your whispers, your Worth line by line. Money, plots and each player's Worth total are public, as in the original.

The big screen is optional. The game runs on the server; the TV and every phone are just screens connected to it. The TV has no seat and no controls, and it never mirrors a phone. Without a TV, each phone shows everything it needs, and the game plays the same (see A phone alone).

Any screen with a browser can be the TV: a laptop, a PC on an HDMI cable, a smart TV's browser. The TV page is drawn at 1280 × 720 and scaled to fit the screen, so it reads the same on any size. The phone page also works on a laptop: from a window 1100 px wide the same screens grow a wider side rail and larger type, and development can be steered with the keyboard.

Why it is like this

From the design doc on big-screen mode: "If a phone ever needs the big screen to make a private decision, or the big screen ever needs a phone to follow the game, the design is wrong."

Joining, the lobby and starting In the game

On a phone

Open the game's address. The first screen, "Pull up a chair", asks for your name and either a room code to join or Start a new game. It also says: "Got a TV or laptop? Open /table on it for the big screen." Whoever starts a new game gets a four-letter room code (no vowels, so codes never spell words) and becomes the host.

In the lobby, step 1 of 2 is "Make your colonist": pick one of the six species and one of the six colours. You are drawn large on the stage, in your colour, with your species' perk. The side rail is the table: "At the table · N of 6", every seat with its creature, name and what it is (a computer player, the host, or AWAY when its phone has gone). A colour a person has taken is theirs; a computer player's colour can be taken, and the computer player moves to another.

The lobby's perks say the game's numbers In the game

Each species card in the lobby says the game's own number, read from the balance settings, so the words follow when the numbers are tuned. Today: the Zipwing "walks 1.25× as fast during development"; the Gearling "pays 95% of the price for MULEs and outfits"; the Bramblet's farms yield +1 Food; the Mollox's mines yield +1, and its geo analysis takes 50% of the time.

Why it is like this

The cards were written before the balance sim and promised more than the game gave: the Zipwing "twice as fast", the Gearling "a quarter less". The balance sim tuned the numbers down (at 1.5× Zipwings won 37% of games, at 75% of the price Gearlings 44%, against 25%), and the words did not follow. Programme manager, 5 Nov: the cards read the numbers themselves.

The host's rail also has Add AI, a ✕ to remove a seat, Close the table and Start. Removing a seat or closing the table asks for one confirming tap first. Start needs at least 2 seats. Anyone else can leave the table before the game starts.

On the TV

Open /table on the big screen. It asks for the room code shown on the host's phone ("Show this table"); a link with the code in it opens the table straight away. The TV lobby shows a large QR code to scan, the join address, the room code in big letters, every seat with its creature and whether it is a computer player, the host, Ready or Away, and "Waiting for the host to start".

With a TV connected, the phone's QR card is small (the TV carries the big one). With no TV, the phone draws its QR big and puts it first, under the next-step card, because it is the only way in.

Why it is like this

Heiko ruled on 21 Oct to keep the compact lobby (version 1 of the approved mocks) and, when no TV is connected, to use the bigger room card with a larger QR in the same spot.

The TV, phase by phase In the game

Every TV screen has the same header: "Month N / 12" above the phase's title, a Day/Night switch, and at the right the colony's gauges (Wealth, Environment, Prestige, Happiness) when the council is on, or the air gauge alone when it is off, and the clock when the phase has one. In planning, where the screen shows a ready count, the gauges take a row of their own under the title; at the month end they sit beside the league instead. The table chat's ticker runs under the header once someone has spoken: the newest line only, on every game screen, the month end and the game's end included. Everything below the header fits the height the header leaves (see The TV fits a real table).

Planning on the TV

The ready list shows each person's place by Worth ("1st", "2nd"), their creature and one word: PLANNING, READY, or AWAY · PLANNING for a dropped phone. Computer players, always ready, share one dimmed row. The map's caption reads the room code, the colonists, the MULEs in the corral and their price. The market tiles show six goods with their price in credits ("156 cr") and how many months of demand the colony holds, the store's stock and every player's together, amber under one month and red under half a month; "no demand" when nobody needs the good next month.

The goods auction on the TV

Each running deal is a row ("Lena buys from the store at 34", for example), newest first. The newest shows its speed: chevrons that fill as the pair stays matched, "faster and faster" until "full speed"; older deals take one row each. Every running deal stays in sight: when they don't all fit beside the board, the side column is drawn smaller, never the board. When no deal runs, a help line explains the board: "Sellers push the red line down, buyers push the green line up. When the two lines meet, the goods change hands." The TV plays the auction's sounds: a tick-tock clock that speeds up as time runs out and slows while trading, a click for every unit, a bell struck three times when a good's walk ends.

Events on the TV

When events strike, the TV plays their show over the phase screen: the header with a pip for each event, the living map where each event lands on its plots, a log of what happened, and a colony bar with every player's slot glowing while their spotlight plays and a "sick" tag once their sickness lands. Production plays its events inside its own show. See Event shows.

The month end opens on the colony grounds Designed, not in the game yet, WS10

When the month ends, the TV plays the colony's grounds full screen at dusk before it shows the league table. The lamps are lit by the colony's Wealth, as it stands after the month. The camera leans in on whatever changed, and one caption names it ("New this month: the Stadium stands"). Then the screen crossfades to the month end as it is today.

  • Order: the month end's event show first, if any events fired (see Who waits). Then the grounds. Then the league table. In a month with no events the grounds play at once.
  • Length: about 6 s when something on the grounds changed that month (a building started, stands or was damaged). Otherwise a 3 s still, and the same 3 s still with reduced motion.
  • Nobody waits for it: the phones show their month end at once and Ready is never refused. If everyone is Ready before it ends, it just stops and the game moves on, so a fast table may never see the league on the TV.
  • The gauges count when the league shows: the four gauges count from last month's numbers to this month's once the month-end screen is on the TV, not when the month ends, so the count never runs unseen behind the grounds.

Why it is like this

The grounds could not be a side panel on the TV's screens; WS4's fit measured 0.593 and 0.662 at six seats, too small to read. One full-screen moment a month fits, and the month end is the one phase without a clock. The event show goes first because it runs on the server's clock in step with the phones; putting the grounds first would hide it on the TV or knock it out of step. A beat at planning was turned down because it would delay the land grant and commissioning. PM ruling, 7 Nov, on WS6's ordering.

Designed, not in the game yet, WS10 (the opener); the gauges' start is a named edit in WS4's code (apps/web/src/components/Colony.tsx::useMonthEndDrift). Stills: WS10's colony-grounds mock, the month-end beat.

The TV fits a real table In the game

Played at a real table, some TV screens ran out of room. Three rules now hold on every TV screen, and every screen below is checked at 1920 × 1080 with three chat lines said: nothing runs off the bottom, every row it promises is shown whole, and nothing is drawn under seven tenths of its size, at six players for the screens of the first two months.

  • The chat strip is one line. Under the header the ticker shows the newest chat line only, on every game screen: planning, election day, the law vote, the land grant, the land auction, development, production, the goods auction's status and walk, the month end and the game's end. Only the lobby would show three, and the TV lobby has no ticker.
  • Every screen's body fits the height under its header. The header (the month, the title, the gauges, the clock and the chat line) stays full size. Everything below it is drawn at full size when it fits; when it would run past the bottom it is drawn smaller as a whole, just small enough to fit, and grows back when room returns. It is never drawn under 0.7 of its full size: below that it can't be read from the couch, so a screen that would need less is a screen to redesign, not to shrink further. At six players, the fullest table, the check holds the screens of a game's first two months to this: planning and election day, the land grant, development, production, the goods auction's status and walk, and the month end; nested parts, such as the column beside the auction's board, count at the size they are drawn on the screen. The game's end, and any screen those two months did not bring up, is checked at three players only. Where a screen has a list that can give way (planning's news), that list gives way first, down to its newest item, and only then does the body shrink.
  • The goods auction's walk shows the one chat line like every other screen, and the board stays one size as deals start and stop. Every running deal stays in sight: the newest keeps its speed line, each older deal is compressed to one row, and none waits out of sight. When they don't all fit, the column beside the board is drawn smaller, as a whole, and the board keeps its size. The help line shows only while no deal runs.
  • Planning: the ready count ("2 / 3 READY") sits beside the title, and the colony's four gauges move to a full-width row of their own under it, so the count never covers a gauge. The news gives way first, down to its newest item, such as the election result, so the last standings row never runs off the screen.
  • The month end: the ready count sits beside the title, and the colony's four gauges sit at the top of the right column, two by two, beside the league, not in a row of their own. The Worth rule takes one line. From five players the league's rows are a step tighter (the rows' type the same size, the leader's line a step smaller), and its small "League table · month N" row goes (the leader's line and the column heads still say what it is). It shows one chat line, as play does: with three, a three-player month end was drawn at about six tenths of its size, and a six-player one less. Who went hungry is one line in the colony panel, however many went hungry: "Went hungry: Lena, Kai, Ossa · ate 0 of 3 Food". When they ate different amounts, each name, or each group that ate the same, keeps its own numbers: "Went hungry: Lena ate 0 of 3 · Kai, Ossa ate 1 of 3 Food". One player alone still reads "Kai went hungry: ate 1 of 3 Food". With everyone hungry, a three-player month end is drawn at about three quarters of its size and a six-player one at 0.73 (measured 6 Nov).
  • Titles stay on one line: "End of the month" no longer wraps, and a long title never runs under the buttons at the header's right.

Why it is like this

A 12-month sweep of every TV screen (6 Nov) found screens with fixed-size content running past the foot under the three-line chat strip: the development map cut every month, production's rail running into the silos with the event card hidden, the election's votes table over the candidates and "Vote on your phone" off the bottom, the law vote's "Laws in force" over the costs, the auction's ship banner cut, and in a strike month the land grant's instruction and the land auction's small map. The programme manager ruled option A on 6 Nov: one chat line in play, three at the month end, and every screen's body fitted to the height left under its header. It supersedes the 5 Nov rulings that took the ticker off the goods auction's walk and cut the month end to one line. A second ruling the same day put the month end and the game's end at one line too, set the floor at 0.7 of full size with six players, and kept the 5 Nov rule that every running deal stays on the TV, the older ones on one row each. A line of its own for each hungry player then drew a month end with everyone hungry under the floor (0.69 of its size with three players, 0.58 with six), so a third ruling that day put the hungry players on one line, their numbers kept true, and kept the floor at 0.7.

Every crowded TV header puts the gauges on their own row In the game

A TV header holds the title, the Day/Night switch, the colony's four gauges and, on some screens, the clock, a status badge (such as a strike) or a ready count. Those don't all fit in one row, so a header that shows a clock, a status badge or a ready count puts the colony's gauges on a full-width row of their own under the title, as planning and the month end do. A header with none of them keeps the gauges beside the title. The map's dots In the game

On the map of the player who did the geology, a known plot with nothing on it shows its best good's icon over 0 to 3 dots, in a chip centred on the plot (in the map's right column a special plot's chip steps left, clear of the special mark); the same good the walking screen calls "Best here" (on a tie, the first of Food, Water, Energy, Smithore, Crystite, Rare earth). The plot's label reads, for example, "Food 2 of 3". A plot with a MULE making a good shows that instead, and a colony building's plot shows the building, not its ground; a Green belt plot makes no good and keeps its dots. So that no mark hides a rating, on a phone the special mark is sized with the plot (at most 15 px), and next to a plot showing its dots (the plot above, or below on the top row), the selection shows no marker pin: its ring alone marks it. On a phone's smaller plots the ring comes in closer, and thins, so it stops short of the dots above; on the smallest plots that still show the full chip (16 to 19 px) it is drawn inside its plot. Under dots its glow comes in too, so even at half strength it stops short of them. On a phone plot under 16 px (the small phone's hub) the chip drops the good's icon and keeps its three dots, in the good's colour, centred even in the right column, so it fits inside the plot; the plot's label still names the good. The TV never shows dots. The scale is in Land. On the small phone (plots of 13 to 15 px) some marks still cover part of the dots: the owner's tab, the gem, the special mark on the bottom-right plot, and the selection on its side neighbours (SA-1372).

The small phone's map: no mark covers the dots Designed, not in the game yet, WS4

On a small phone (667 × 375) the hub's map draws plots only 13 to 15 px wide, and the marks on a plot (the owner's tab, the gem, a special's badge, the selection ring and the SALE, SCAN and OFF flags) used to cover part of its dots. Under 16 px, on a plot that shows its dots:

  • The dots sit at the plot's foot. On bigger plots they stay centred.
  • The other marks share the strip above them: the owner's tab, the gem and a special's badge, each at least as big as a dot (3 px), none touching the dots. If they don't all fit, the badge goes first, then the gem. The owner's coloured border always stays.

The phone: always sideways In the game

The phone is played held sideways for the whole game. Held upright it shows only "turn your phone"; the game stays loaded underneath, so nothing is lost while you turn it. The screens are drawn for a phone of 844 × 390 and still fit a small one of 667 × 375.

Every phone screen has the same frame. The stage on the left holds the phase's content: the map, the board, the hall. The rail on the right holds, from the top:

  • the month ("Month 3 / 12") and the phase's name, with the clock when the phase has one, or a ready count when it has none;
  • your creature and name, the Day/Night switch, "Move to my phone", and your money; in planning, under your money, your place ("2nd of 4"), which opens the standings;
  • Now: the one next step and why;
  • the screen's own controls;
  • the table chat: everyone's seat and the last two lines;
  • at the bottom, within thumb reach, the main action.

Why it is like this

Heiko, 28 Oct: "phone is always landscape btw and the steering control should be laying over the card", then "Whole game" when asked whether that meant every screen. This replaced the design doc's earlier "Phones stay portrait".

Planning on the phone: the hub In the game

Planning opens the month's hub (the team calls its built parts "Today"). The stage holds:

  • The month's roadmap: Plan, Land grant, Land auction (only in a month with a lot), Develop, Produce, Goods auction, this step lit, and one plain line on what to do now and what comes next. With the council on, the offices sit under it.
  • What you need this month: Food, Energy and Water against what you hold, red where you are short. A tap opens the Colony desk.
  • This month: everything the month allows, each a door into a desk or a map tool. It heads with the export ship ("Export ship in month 6: it buys the colony's leftovers", or "in port"). Its items: Next month's vote, Throw your hat in the ring (before an election), Geo drone, Sell a plot or MULE, and the Governor's desk for the Governor.
  • The colony map with its tools: Look (tap a plot to see what it can make and whose it is), Geo drone (pick up to 2 free plots and send it) and Sell a plot (tap your plot or one with your MULE).
  • Under the map: the store's prices and stock in one row of goods.

The rail holds Now, the open tool's panel, and the desks: Colony (what you need, the news, the colony), Talk (the table chat, with an unread badge), Council (with the council on), Market (put a plot or MULE up; the month's line-up of lots) and, for the Governor, the Governor's desk (set the tax, propose a law). In an election month, election day takes the stage until the votes are over.

At the foot, "I'm ready". Once you are, it shows who the table still waits for; the host can then continue without them after one confirming tap ("End planning for everyone now? … stop where they are, and the land grant starts.").

Development on the sideways phone In the game

In development the phone becomes an arcade controller. The whole colony map fills the screen, with every colonist walking live. A translucent stick lies over the bottom-left corner and the action button over the bottom-right. When you walk into the store, the stage becomes the store's hall: six shopfronts for the raw goods along the top, the works for the factories, the corral and the pub.

  • The button says what it will do where you stand: BUY MULE, ANALYSE, FIT FILTER, TAKE MULE, CATCH MULE, FIGHT, GRAB THE GLINT, PUB · END TURN and so on. It is dim when there is nothing to do. The server decides; a press is a request.
  • A ring shows your next step: the store and its corral when you have no MULE, the outfitters you can pay when it is empty, then the best of your free plots for its good. When you cannot afford a MULE, it points to what you can do instead: analyse a plot, or the pub.
  • The side rooms beside the map hold the clock, your money and your pace line, for example "Your pace 47%: you ate 1 of the 3 Food you need", with a tap for what to do about it next month. On a small phone the status lies in a bar over the map's top edge.
  • When your turn ends, a card says "In the pub: your turn is over" or "Development over", with how many MULEs you installed and how long the others still walk.
  • If no update has arrived for 1 s, the phone shows "reconnecting" and stops guessing where you are.

On a laptop, the arrow keys steer and SPACE presses the button; you can also hold the mouse on a spot to walk there and click the lit thing to act.

The sick line on the sideways phone In the game

When you are sick, the pace line in the walking screen's side room says that you are sick, why, and your pace this turn:

  • the sickness and its cause, with a cross in the Medicine colour: "✚ Sick: the air is toxic.", "… your own pollution fouled the air.", or "Sick this month.";
  • then "50% pace this turn", adding "· you ate 2 of 5 Food" when you are hungry too;
  • a tap adds that it struck as your turn began with no Medicine to cure it, and that a Medicine in hand when a turn begins stops it (it is used up);
  • on a small phone the bar reads "✚ Sick · 50% pace".

Why it is like this

The PM's ruling A, 5 Nov: the line says it and why, in the place you already look for your pace, rather than a banner competing with the guide's first note. Before it, the line only said "Pace 50%: sick". The tap screen's start card, which explained sickness, went with the tap screen.

Chat, whispers, emotes and bubbles In the game

You type in the Talk desk in planning: to the whole table, or whispered to one player ("WHISPER · ONLY YOU AND KAI SEE THIS"). Five quick emotes sit above the box: Deal?, Nice!, Traitor!, Ha! and Help!. A line holds up to 280 characters. A whisper reaches only its two ends, never the TV; a whisper between two people stays hidden from the computer players too.

Every screen, the TV included, shows what was said:

  • A speech bubble pops at the speaker's creature wherever the screen draws it (on the map, on the auction board, in the store's hall). Where the screen does not draw the speaker, the bubble opens at their place in the seat row. One bubble shows at a time on each screen, so they never cover each other: each shows for at least 2 s and at most 4.5 s, and a line that would wait more than 4 s for its turn gets no bubble. A new phase ends every bubble. A bubble opens above its speaker, or below or beside where above would cover a name, a caption or a price, sliding along its side to stay clear and inside its screen; on a crowded screen it takes the place that covers least.
  • The ticker keeps the last lines, newest first: two on a phone (in the rail, under its content); on the TV (under the header) the newest line only, on every game screen, the month end and the game's end included (see The TV fits a real table). A whisper is marked 🔒.
  • Emotes float at the top of every phone's stage for 3.5 s.

Computer players talk too: each says at most 3 lines a month and one a phase, up to 140 characters, about what it just decided. A public line from a person reaches every computer player, a whisper only the one it was for.

Why it is like this

Heiko, 22 Oct: live table chat "Live on every screen", a speech bubble at the speaker and a short ticker on the phones and the TV in every phase. He approved the design the same day (ticker 2 lines on a phone, 3 on the TV; whispers only on the two screens involved, with a lock, never on the TV), with the PM's condition that bubbles never cover each other or the ticker.

What is changing

Only the planning hub has a place to type. In every other phase you can read the chat and see bubbles but not write. This is intended (UX ruling C20): in development and the auction your hands are on the controls and the side rail has no room. One-tap phrases on the auction status screen and in the pub are in the backlog. Owner: the UX team (WS6).

A phone alone In the game

The game plays without a TV. A phone on its own (one person against computer players, or several people each on their own phone with no big screen) notices that no TV is connected and takes over what the TV would show:

  • The lobby draws the room's QR big, first on the screen.
  • Every phase already shows the whole map: the hub's colony map, the land grant's lit plot, the arcade's whole colony, the auction board.
  • Production and events play their full show on the phone, and a tap skips ahead, instead of the light card and the buzz.
  • The month end fits the league table and the colony panel on one stage.
  • Game over shows the colony's report under your result.

This follows the TV live: if the TV disconnects mid-game, the phones switch to these screens; when it comes back, they switch back.

Dropping out and getting back in In the game

  • A dropped connection: the phone shows "CONNECTION LOST · REJOINING…" and tries again by itself, waiting a little longer each time, up to 5 s between tries. It takes its seat back with the seat's own key.
  • A reload: the phone remembers its seat and takes it back by itself, unless you opened a link to another table.
  • A new device: "Move to my phone" (in every game screen's rail, and in the lobby) shows a QR and a one-time code. Opened on the other device within 2 minutes, it moves your seat there, with your money, goods and plots, and the old screen steps aside ("You're playing on another device now"). The game does not pause meanwhile.
  • Nobody new can join a game that has started. A seat whose device is lost and has no saved key cannot be taken back.
  • The host passes to the next connected person while the host's phone is gone, and back when it returns.
  • A room that nobody has been connected to for 30 minutes is closed.

While you are away, the table treats you by phase: planning, the votes, the goods auction's side pick and the month end wait for you (the host can go on without you); in the land grant you just do not claim; your colonist stands still in development; in the walks you stop where you stand; production does not wait for you. The TV and the other phones mark you AWAY.

Reduced motion and accessibility In the game

  • Reduced motion: on a device set to reduce motion, the event shows' looping effects hold still and their entrances become a plain fade; the store and the MULEs stop animating; creatures stop their walking cycle and hold a still lean; the show's vibrate icon stops shaking. The production show, the election reveal and the event shows all follow the setting live.
  • Telling players apart: every player has both a colour and a species shape, so colour is never the only clue. Changes in the league table carry an arrow and a sign (↑ +120, ↓ −40), never colour alone.
  • Day and Night: every screen has a Day/Night switch; the phone remembers its choice.
  • Screen readers: the chat ticker is announced as it changes; private lines carry an "only you see this" label; the creature in your header is named ("You, Bramblet").
  • Keyboard: on a laptop, development is played with the arrow keys and SPACE.
  • Readable from the couch: the TV is drawn for 3 metres: large type, few words.

The design doc's host Pause button (for dinner and phone calls, shown on the TV) is not built: the game never pauses, and a move to another phone says so. Owner: the Table team (WS4).

The colony on the TV: mood drift and strikes In the game

With the council on, the TV shows two things the colony does on its own: the mood drifting at the month end, and a general strike.

The month end's mood drift

At the month end, the TV's Happiness gauge opens on last month's value and, about 1.2 s later, counts to this month's. When the count crosses into another band (Restless into Unhappy, for example), the band's name flips and the gauge pulses. It shows no reasons, only "↓ n this month". It plays once, as the TV sees the month end arrive; a TV that reloads into the month end, or a device set to reduce motion, shows the end state at once. Nothing plays in month 1, with an unchanged mood, or with the council off.

Strikes

During a general strike, the TV header carries a strike badge ("STRIKE · Food 20%", then "PAID · −250 cr" once paid), the TV shows whose choice the strike waits on ("Waiting for Kai, the Governor"), and strikers with placards stand over every plot of the struck good on the map. The Governor's phone shows the strike in its Now card, with "Pay the strikers" and "Let them strike"; everyone else's phone shows a line saying the strike is on and who decides.

The strike badge is one of the status badges that move the colony's gauges to a row of their own under the title (the gauges row). Development is the exception: its gauges stay beside the title, so the map keeps its size.

Why it is like this

The Events team (WS8) built both in the colony branch (mood drift #365, strikes #368), with the Table team's OK on the TV header. The strike rules themselves came with C26 (PR #445). All of it is in the game.
