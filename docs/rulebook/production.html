Production

After development every plot with a MULE works for a month. This chapter says what each plot makes and why: the ground, the factories, the Energy and Water every plot needs, what spoils, what your crew eats, how industry fouls the land and the air, and how the TV and your phone show it all.

What happens in production, step by step In the game

Production runs once a month, between development and the goods auction. Nobody acts during it: the whole table watches the production show while the game works through a fixed order. The order matters, because each step can only use what the steps before it left in your depot.

StepWhat happensWhy it comes here

3For each player, the game checks whether the Energy in their depot covers all their other plots. If not, it turns plots off, the biggest Energy user first, until the rest fit (see Energy). Everything you made lands in your depot at once. There is no buying or selling during production: Energy or Water you are short of now can only be bought at the goods auction that follows, for next month.

What this means at the table

The goods auction comes after production. Energy you buy there is held over the month end, loses a quarter to spoilage, and powers next month's plots. So the Energy that matters for this month's production is what you held when the month started plus what your own Energy plots make now.

What a farm, well, plant or mine makes In the game

The six raw goods come out of the ground. Food, Water and Energy are grown; Smithore, Crystite and Rare earth are mined. A plot's output is built up from a base and a list of pluses and minuses, always in the same order, and the game keeps every part so it can tell you why a plot made what it made.

The base: the ground

  • Grown goods. A farm, well or Energy plot makes GROWN_BASE (2) plus the plot's rating for that good, but never less than GROWN_MIN (1). Ratings run from −2 to +3, so the base runs from 1 to 5. A poor plot still works, just badly.
  • Mined goods. A mine makes exactly its deposit rating, 0 to 4. A deposit of 0 means there is nothing to mine: the plot makes 0 and no bonus of any kind is added.

The rating is rolled once per plot when the map is made, from odds set by the terrain, and stays hidden until you analyse it. The Land and geology chapter has the odds.

Traits that change nothing yet

A plot can carry other traits: bedrock, sheltered, unstable, infested and flood plain. The settings say these "only change event risks", but no event reads them today, so in the game they change nothing at all.

What is changing

Open: what bedrock, sheltered, unstable, infested and flood plain should do. The event model reads no plot trait (events.ts has none of them), so the comment on TRAIT_EFFECT describes a plan, not the game. Owner of the answer: the Balance team (WS1), with the Maps team (WS5) for the traits themselves.

Why it is like this

Grown goods always make at least 1, so cheap land is never worthless; mined goods make exactly what is in the ground, so a 0 deposit is a real loss and assays matter. Both rules are in the design's starting numbers. The neighbour bonus rewards building clusters, and the minus for pollution makes industry next to farms a cost the farmer pays.

Factories and recipes In the game

Three goods are made in factories from other goods. A factory takes its whole recipe from its owner's depot in one go. If any input is missing, Energy included, the factory makes nothing that month, takes nothing, and its run in a row starts again at 0.

Which ground suits which factory

Every terrain not listed is neutral for that factory. Plains are neutral for a smelter and a lab (the phone says "Plains suit any factory") and good ground for an Electronics works.

Example: a Medicine lab that runs well

Kai's Medicine lab stands on forest (+1, "Herbs nearby"). It touches his own well (a feeder: its Energy drops from 1 to 0) and another Medicine lab of his (industrial park, +1). It has run 4 months in a row (experience, +1). A neighbour's smelter has raised the plot's pollution to level 1 (−1). Output: 2 + 1 + 1 + 1 − 1 = 5 Medicine, for 1 Food and 1 Water and no Energy.

Ruins pay once

Ruins are a special field that makes nothing by itself. At the end of the first production after someone owns them, they pay their owner their salvage and become a plain plot: 2 Alloy or 1 Electronics, whichever the map drew.

Why it is like this

Factories are where the money is (Electronics' usual price is 220), so they cost: 2 crew instead of 1, inputs every month, and they run last in a shortage. The feeder, park and experience bonuses reward building an industrial estate in one place and keeping it running, which is also where pollution builds up.

Energy: what each plot uses, and what happens when you run short In the game

Heiko set the Energy rules on 5 November; they replaced the old halving rule (how it changed). In his words: "if i am not having enugh energy i first turn off the plot that uses most until i end up with enoguh energy. enrgey prodcuing plots use energy as well ... A plot that is turned off only produces 20%".

The rules in one place

  1. Every plot uses Energy, by what it produces: the table below. Energy plants use Energy too.
  2. Energy plants produce first and pay their own use from their own output. They are never turned off, because that would only deepen the shortage.
  3. If your Energy can't cover all your other plots, the plot that uses most is turned off first, then the next biggest, until the plots still running fit what you have.
  4. A turned-off plot produces 20% of its normal output and uses no Energy.
  5. The plot's line says why: "Turned off: not enough Energy (20%)".

Each working plot uses this much Energy a month, paid during production. A cold snap (a colony event, 3.2% a month) doubles every plot's use for that month.

What factories need

A factory turns goods from your own stock into something worth more. Each run takes its inputs and its Energy together.

FactoryTakes per runEnergyMakes
Alloy smelter2 Smithore22 Alloy
Electronics works1 Rare earth, 1 Alloy11 Electronics
Medicine lab1 Food, 1 Water12 Medicine

Ground, a neighbouring factory of the same kind, experience and relics can raise what a factory makes; see Factories and recipes. Under the new rule:

  • A factory that holds its inputs is in the turn-off list like any other plot. Factories set their inputs aside in turn (smelters, then works, then labs, each in map order), so two smelters sharing Smithore for one count as one: the other waits, and no mine is turned off to make room for it.
  • A factory whose inputs only arrive this month (Smithore mined this month for your smelter) runs after your farms, wells and mines, on whatever Energy is left. It never causes another plot to be turned off. If too little Energy is left, it is turned off itself.
  • A turned-off factory makes 20% of its output and uses 20% of each input, rounded up. If 20% of its output rounds to nothing, it takes no inputs.
  • A factory missing its inputs doesn't run and doesn't count.

Which plot is turned off first

  • The plot that uses the most Energy goes first.
  • When two use the same, the one whose output is worth less at the store's current price goes first, so your most valuable plot keeps running.
  • Still tied: map order.
  • Your farms, wells, mines and factories are all in one list. Each player's plots are settled on their own.
  • A turned-off plot gains no experience that month.

20% is rounded to the nearest whole unit, halves up: a plot making 3 or 4 still gives 1; a plot making 2 or less gives 0.

Example

Lena holds no Energy. She owns one Energy plant making 3, a Crystite mine (makes 3), a Smithore mine (makes 3), farm A (makes 3) and farm B (makes 4). Prices: Smithore 50, Crystite 100.

  1. The plant makes 3 and pays its own 1: 2 Energy go into her stock.
  2. Her other plots need 2 + 2 + 1 + 1 = 6. She has 2. Too little.
  3. The two mines use most, 2 each. The Smithore mine's output is worth 3 × 50 = 150, the Crystite mine's 3 × 100 = 300, so the Smithore mine is turned off first. The rest need 4: still too much.
  4. The Crystite mine is turned off next. The rest need 2: that fits.
  5. Both farms work in full and use her 2 Energy. Each mine makes 20% of 3 = 0.6, rounded to 1.

Result: farms 3 and 4, mines 1 and 1. Under the old rule the farms' place on the map decided it, and farm B made only 2.

Why it is like this

Heiko, 5 Nov: the biggest users stop first, so a shortage is predictable and the cheap staples keep running, and a plant that pays its own Energy makes Energy a real cost of every estate. The PM settled the details on 5 Nov: plants pay from their own output and are never turned off; ties go by value; factories in the same list; inputs rounded up so nothing is made from nothing; no experience while off.

The phone and the TV show a turned-off plot with a red "20%" badge and the Energy icon, and the words "Turned off: not enough Energy (20%)". The computer players price a missing unit of Energy at what the plot it keeps on would lose turned off: 80% of that plot's output at the store's price, spread over the plot's use.

The old rule: half, in map order

Each farm, well and mine, in map order, asked for its Energy. If the depot held enough it paid and worked in full; if not, it paid nothing and made half its output, rounded down. Factories came after every raw plot and made nothing without their full Energy. Measured on 5 Nov (WS9, 40 games on each of 3 seeds): in 40 to 43% of seat-months a seat lost production this way, 33 to 40% of the month's output value. That is why Heiko ruled the change.

Short of Water today, until the new rule lands: farms make half In the game

Food farms need irrigation. When production starts, the game compares the Water you hold with the Water your household and crew need this month (see Upkeep and crew). If you hold less, every one of your Food farms makes half, rounded down. This comes on top of any Energy shortfall: a farm short of both makes a quarter, rounded down at each step.

  • Only Food farms are irrigated. Wells, Energy plots and mines don't care about Water.
  • The check is made once, before any plot runs. Water your wells make this month counts for next month.
  • The Water is not taken at this point. You pay it at the month end, when your household and crew drink.

Why it is like this

Heiko's ruling of 21 October 2026 (economy design): "Short of Water, your Food farms make half, because they can't irrigate." It makes Water a real need for a Food grower and gives wells a reason to exist beyond the colonists' demand.

Events during production In the game

Production is the busiest phase for events. Its events roll at the start of the phase and act at the end of each plot's line: after the ground, the bonuses, pollution and any shortage. Several can hit one plot; each works on what the ones before it left, and no plot goes below 0.

  • Events only reach farms, wells, Energy plots and mines. Factories are never hit by a production event, except a meteor strike.
  • Energy plots are credited first, events included: a dust storm leaves you less Energy for your other plots that same month, and a solar flare more.
  • A cold snap does not touch output directly; it doubles every plot's Energy need, which can push you into a shortage.
  • A crystal cavern that was mined for Crystite may collapse after production (6% each), and a meteor may strike after it (3.2%).

The Events chapter has every event, its chance and its numbers.

What is changing

A general strike, announced in planning when the colony is restless, will bite in production: every plot of the struck good makes 20% of its output, rounded down, unless the Governor pays the strikers. Being built in the colony branch (C26 PR-A, PR #370, the Balance team WS1). See the colony's social events.

Spoilage and storage In the game

At the month end, after you have eaten and drunk, your goods spoil and anything above your home depot's space is lost. Each step rounds down.

  1. Each good loses its spoilage share: what is left is your stock × (1 − spoilage), rounded down.
  2. Your home depot holds UPKEEP.depotCapacity = 10 units of each good. Anything above that is gone.
GoodLost each month (yours)Lost each month (the store's)
Food50%12.5%
Water5%1.25%
Energy25%6.25%
Medicine20%5%
Smithore, Crystite, Rare earth, Alloy, Electronics0%0%

The store has proper storage, so its goods spoil at PRICE.storeSpoilage = 0.25 of the normal rate, and it has no depot limit.

Example: holding Food and Energy over the month end

After eating, Kai holds 7 Food, 4 Energy and 14 Smithore. Food: 7 × 0.5 = 3.5, rounded down to 3. Energy: 4 × 0.75 = 3. Smithore doesn't spoil, but the depot holds 10: 4 Smithore are lost. Selling the extra Smithore at the auction was his decision to make.

Why it is like this

The economy design: holding goods to sell later is allowed, but storing them costs space and spoilage, so every speculation is a real bet. Spoilage rates are the design's starting numbers.

What is changing

The storage buildings of the economy design (warehouse, silo, cold store, tank, battery bank, vault) are not in the game: the engine has none of them. Backlog, no team on it.

Upkeep and crew In the game

Every plot you work has a crew, and your household and crew eat and drink at the month end. A bigger estate costs more to feed.

PlotCrew
Farm, well, Energy plot or mine1
Factory2
Green belt0
  • Food a month: 2 for you, plus 1 for every 4 crew or part of 4.
  • Water a month: 1 for you, plus 1 for every 6 crew or part of 6.

At the month end both are taken from your depot; if you don't have enough, you go down to 0. Being short of Food shows up at the next development, where a hungry colonist walks slower (see Development). Being short of Water shows up at the next production, where your farms make half. Example: what an estate eats

Five raw plots and one factory: a crew of 5 + 2 = 7. Food: 2 + 2 (7 crew is one full 4 and part of another) = 4. Water: 1 + 2 = 3. To keep your farms irrigated next month you need to hold 3 Water when production starts.

Pollution and the air gauge In the game

Industry fouls the land and the air. Pollution has two levels: each plot has its own level, and the colony shares one air gauge. Both move once a month, right after production.

Who pollutes

PlotAir points a monthWith a filterFouls the plots around it

Electronics works10.5No
Smithore mine10.5No

A plot pollutes because a MULE is installed on it, whether or not it produced: the game counts every outfitted plot, so a smelter that stood idle for want of Smithore still adds its 3 points. A filter costs 1 Alloy and is fitted in development.

Pollution on a plot

  • A polluter that fouls raises its own plot and the four touching it by 1 level each month, up to level 3, whoever owns them. The store's plot never takes pollution.
  • Every other plot recovers by 1 level a month. Untouched forest and swamp (no MULE on it) recovers by 2.
  • A green belt (a MULE outfit costing 20 cr) makes nothing, but cleans its own plot and the four touching it by 1 more each month, and takes 2 points off the air.
  • Food, Water and Medicine made on a plot lose 1 per level. Mines, Energy plots, smelters and Electronics works don't mind.

The colony's air gauge

The gauge runs from 0 to 100. Each month it adds every polluter's points, takes off 2 for each green belt, then nature cleans 2 points for each player in the game (a bigger colony has a bigger map). It never goes below 0 or above 100, and can hold half points because of filters.

The air points each player caused this month are recorded. They make that player likelier to fall sick next month: 3 points of chance per air point, at most 40 (see Events).

Example: does the air move?

Four players, so nature cleans 8 a month. On the map: one Rare earth mine (4), one Alloy smelter with a filter (1.5), two Smithore mines (2), one green belt (−2). The total is 4 + 1.5 + 2 − 2 − 8 = −2.5: the air gets cleaner. With three Rare earth mines and two unfiltered smelters instead (12 + 6 = 18), the gauge rises by 10 a month and reaches Smoggy in three months.

Why it is like this

From the pollution design: one player can get rich from a dirty mine while the whole table pays for it, which makes the colony's health something to deal, vote and argue about. The design said nature cleans 3 a month; the game cleans 2 per player, because a bigger colony has a bigger map. Measured on 28 October (WS1, 120 computer-player games): the gauge never reached Smoggy in any game; the highest month anywhere was 22. Computer players rarely build enough heavy industry to foul the air.

The production show on the TV and the phone In the game

Production is played as a show, in the order Heiko chose on 5 November ("order C"): good by good for every player at once, then every bonus at once, then every loss at once, then the month's events, then the goods fly into the silos. The show's length is production's length: it grows with the number of goods made, never with the number of plots.

BeatWhat you seeHow long
IntroThe map, before the first good lights.1.0 s
HarvestOne good at a time (Food, Water, Energy, Smithore, Crystite, Rare earth, Alloy, Electronics, Medicine), only the goods made this month. Every plot of that good lights and counts up its base together, a unit every 0.14 s.1.1 s a good
BonusesEvery plus at once: neighbours, species, special fields. Green badges.1.6 s
LossesEvery minus at once: pollution in amber, an Energy or Water shortage as a red "½" badge with the missing good.1.6 s
This month on IrataThe month's production events play over the map, one after the other (see the event show). Skipped in a quiet month.their own length
Into the silosEvery plot's goods fly to its owner's silo, the first gaps longest.3.15 s
HoldThe silos stand full to be read.2.0 s

A short gap of 0.5 s sits before the bonuses, before the events and before the flight.

Example: how long a month's show lasts

A month where five goods were made and nothing fired: 1.0 + 5 × 1.1 + 0.5 + 1.6 + 1.6 + 0.5 + 3.15 + 2.0 = about 15.9 s. Add one drought (a headline: 1.8 s intro + 7.0 s) and it is about 25.2 s.

On the TV

The whole map plays, every player's plots. Beside it a rail lists the beats (Harvest, Bonuses, Losses, This month on Irata, Into the silos) and ticks them off. During Bonuses and Losses it lists, in words, the reasons that appear this month, for example "Next to your own plot of the same good", "Polluted ground", "A farm with no Water makes half", "No Energy for the plot: half". During the events it keeps a log of what played. A silo bar along the bottom fills as the goods land.

On the phone

Your phone plays the same map but fills only your plots, and your harvest flies into "Your silo", a strip showing everything you hold. The rail lists what you made, good by good (on a phone alone, once the show ends). With a TV in the room the phone shows each event as a light card and buzzes as it is announced. Alone, with no TV connected, the phone plays each event on its own map, and a tap on the map skips to the result.

Ready waits for the end of the show, so the table always sees its month. With a TV in the room every person can press "Speed up"; once everyone has, the rest of the show plays 3 times faster. Computer players always agree.

Why a plot made what it made In the game

For every working plot the game files a line in the month's report: the plot, the good, what it made, and every part that made it up, in order. The parts always add up exactly to what the plot made.

PartMeaningOn screen
groundThe base from the ground rating or deposit.The base the plot counts up to in the harvest.
factoryA factory's whole run (its base and every fit line).The base the plot counts up to.
traitHot spring, toxic ground or a thin vein.A badge with the change.
speciesThe Bramblet's Food or the Mollox's mining perk."Your species is good at this", +1.
specialA crystal cavern, geyser, oasis or touching relic."A special field".

eventOne event's change, naming the event.Played in the events beat, not as a badge.

Every screen sees the same lines: what each plot made is public, like the auction's status table. The lines are also what the simulator and the computer players read. A factory's own reasons (terrain, feeder, park, experience, relic, pollution) are shown in words on the phone before you install it, not in the month's line.
