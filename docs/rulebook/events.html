Events

Storms, pests, pirates, inheritances and strikes: the things that happen to the colony and to you by chance. This chapter says how an event is rolled, every event in the game with its chance and its numbers, how the TV and your phone play them, and the colony's mood events that are being built now.

How events work In the game

Heiko's model, in his words (28 October): "We just throw a dice for each event and see whether it fires. So we can have multiple events happening in the same turn." Every event has its own row in the game's settings and its own dice.

  • Colony events and player events. A colony event rolls once for the whole colony. A player event rolls once for each player, and hits only that player's land, cash or stock. Colony events roll first, in table order; then each player's events, seat by seat.
  • Each event belongs to a phase. It rolls at the start of that phase: planning, development, production, the goods auction or the month end. The land grant and the land auction have no events today. MULE theft is the one event that rolls in two phases.
  • Several can fire at once. Each row rolls on its own, so a month can bring none, one or several events, even in the same phase.
  • No events in month 1. The rolled events start in month EVENTS.firstMonth = 2: in month 1 there is nothing developed to hit. Sickness is the exception and rolls from month 1.
  • Some pairs never fire together. Drought and flood, and dust storm and solar flare: if both roll in one phase, the pair's own dice keep one of them.
  • An event needs something to hit. A personal event starts on that player's land; if they have no plot it could hit (a pest swarm with no farm), it does not fire. Rats and the crooked accountant fire only when they would take at least 1.
  • Events that roll after production's output. A meteor strike rolls once, after every plot has produced. A cavern collapse rolls once for each crystal cavern that was mined for Crystite this month, also after the output.
  • Replays. Every roll is seeded from the game's seed, the month, the event, the phase and whose roll it is, so a game replays exactly, and retuning one event never moves another's dice. A computer player's look-ahead never sees events.

Why it is like this

Heiko, 28 October: "We distinguish player events from colony events. each event has a likelihood", then "or we have events per phase". Before this, at most one event fired a month, from one dice, always in production, and events came in about 45% of months. One row per event means a new event or a retune is a line in the settings, not code. Heiko's four rulings of 28 October on the catalogue: keep the catalogue's amount of events; count a player's own pollution per air point; build wave 1, then wave 2, then the colony's mood events, then the rest; and lean bad luck toward the richest and good luck toward the poorest.

How likely an event is In the game

Each row has a base chance for its phase. Things that make it likelier or less likely are added as points (a point is one percent), and the result is held between 0 and the row's cap, 90% for every event today.

chance = base + air band + own pollution + wealth + follows, never below 0, never above 90%

InfluenceWhat it readsUsed by
Air bandThe colony's air gauge band: Clean, Smoggy, Toxic or Collapse.Sickness, drought, acid rain
Own pollutionThe air points the player's own plots caused last month (a filter halves them), so many points each, up to a cap.Sickness
WealthWhether the player is the richest or the poorest, by cash plus 300 per plot owned. A tie goes to the first seat.Pest swarm, wildfire, bumper harvest
FollowsWhether another event fired earlier in this phase, or last month. It counts once even if both.Flood, wildfire

Example: how likely is Kai to fall sick?

The air is Smoggy (gauge 34). Last month Kai's Rare earth mine and his unfiltered smelter caused 7 air points. Sickness: base 3 + Smoggy 15 + 7 × 3 = 21 = 39%. Had he fitted filters, his points would have been 3.5, worth 10.5: about 29%. In Clean air with no industry he would be at 3%.

Example: a fire after a drought

Lena is the richest player. Last month a drought hit the colony. Wildfire for Lena: base 2.4 + richest 1 + follows a drought 3 = 6.4%. For the other players it is 2.4% + 3 = 5.4%.

Production's events In the game

Production's events change what plots make. They act last on each plot's line, after the ground, the bonuses, pollution and any shortage; several can hit the same plot, each on what the ones before it left, never below 0. They only reach farms, wells, Energy plots and mines, never factories (a meteor strike is the exception). See Production.

How the TV and the phone show events In the game

Every event plays its own story where it happens: a dust wall sweeps in from its edge, the river swells and spills, a pirate ship lands at the store and carries the Crystite up its ramp, the store burns shelf by shelf. The show is a pure function of the server's clock, so a phone that reconnects lands in the same place as everyone else.

What plays, and for how long

When a phase fires events, its show opens with a short card of what fired, then plays the events in the order they fired. Each event has a weight that sets how big its story is.

WeightHow it playsLengthEvents
IntroThe phase's opening card of what fired. None in a quiet phase.1.8 sEvery phase with an event
HeadlineThe full story over the whole map.7.0 sDust storm, drought, acid rain, solar flare, comet pass, flood season, volcanic ash, Federation tax, Federation grant, immigration wave, epidemic, cold snap, meteor strike, pirate raid, store fire. Any event the table forgets plays as a headline.
SpotlightA shorter story on the plots it hits.5.2 sPest swarm, wildfire, bumper harvest, cavern collapse, MULE theft
BulletinOne line in a roll call that every bulletin of the phase shares.4.0 s sharedInheritance, rats in the silo, crooked accountant, package from home, sickness

A phase's events play for at most 30 s in all. Past that, the intro keeps its length and every event's share is cut in the same proportion.

Example: a busy development

An epidemic fires, and two players fall sick. Show: intro 1.8 s + epidemic 7.0 s + one roll call 4.0 s naming both sick players = 12.8 s. Development's clock starts when the show ends.

Who waits

  • Land grant, land auction, development, goods auction: every clock of the phase starts when the show ends. Until then nothing can be done; a press is refused with "The event is still playing".
  • Planning: not held. The events play over the hub while people act.
  • Production: its events play in the production show's "This month on Irata" beat, and production grows by their length (see the production show).
  • The month end: not held today, because it has no clock.

On the TV

The TV plays every story on the big map. A row of badges, one per event of the phase, lights the one playing and ticks off the ones done. A log beside the map keeps what is playing now and what already happened, in words. Changes off the map (cash, Crystite, colonists, the store's shelves) show as chips on each player's slot and the colony bar.

The colony's social events in planning Being built

When politics is on, the colony has a mood, from 0 to 100, starting at 60. Its band is Thriving (80 and up), Content (60 to 79), Restless (40 to 59), Unhappy (20 to 39) or Unrest (below 20); see Colony and politics. The first set of social events lets the mood tilt the odds: a happy colony throws festivals, an unhappy one strikes and leaves. They roll in planning, use the same dice and influences as every other event, and add two more: the mood band, and a hard "never in" list of bands where the event cannot fire whatever raises it. With politics off they never fire.

The general strike, step by step

  1. Planning: the strike is announced on one good. Everyone sees it.
  2. Still in planning, once the votes are over, the Governor may pay the strikers COLONY.strike.cost = 250 cr from the colony fund. The fund's reserve does not hold this back: paying may empty the fund. Nobody else can pay; with no Governor the strike holds.
  3. Production: if unpaid, every plot of the struck good makes COLONY.strike.output = 0.2 of its output, rounded down, and the production show plays a "General strike" event on those plots. If paid, everything runs in full and only a news line says so.

Example: a Food strike

The colony is Unhappy and Food sells at 50 (above 1.5 × 30 = 45). Strike chance: 0 + 5 + 3 = 8%. It fires on Food, the good most plots make. Lena governs and the fund holds 400 cr. If she pays, the fund falls to 150 and every farm works. If not, a farm that would have made 4 makes 0.8, rounded down to 0, and one that would have made 6 makes 1.

Why it is like this

From the colony design (G-5): running the colony badly has visible consequences (strikes, theft, emigration), and happiness tilts the odds of social events, not the overall event rate. The Governor's choice to pay is G-5's rule 26. Measured in the build (40 games on each of 3 seeds, politics on): strikes fired 10 to 16 times in Restless months and 5 to 7 in Unhappy ones, and no game reached Thriving or Unrest, so festivals were rare (1 to 4). The tuning belongs to the colony's balance pass at the end.

Strikes, mood events and gauges on screen In the game

The colony's events show on the TV and the phones. Each event's story plays when the event itself rolls in the game. With politics off, or with no strike, every screen stays as it is without them.

The general strike

  • TV header: a badge beside the month: "STRIKE · Food 20%" while unpaid, "PAID · −250 cr" once paid. No strike, no badge. While the badge shows, the colony's gauges sit on a row of their own under the title, as they do beside a ready count.
  • TV planning map and the phone's Look map: in planning, a striker with a placard on every plot of the struck good; on the TV each also carries a "STRIKE · 20%" chip. They leave once the strike is paid.
  • The Governor's phone: once the votes are over, the Now card is the strike: "General strike on Food", "Unpaid: Food plots make 20% this month.", the fund before and after (Fund 400 → 150 cr), then Pay the strikers 250 cr and Let them strike. While an election or a law ballot is open, the vote keeps the Now card. Let them strike sends nothing to the game: the card turns into "You let them strike" and keeps a pay button until planning ends. Paid, it reads "You paid the strikers · −250 cr".
  • A Governor who is already Ready: the Now card belongs to the ready bar, so a pay line, "Pay the Food strikers 250 cr", sits under Now instead, until the strike is paid or planning ends.
  • Everyone else's phone: an alert line under Now: "General strike on Food: Kai decides whether the fund pays 250 cr", then "Kai pays the Food strikers 250 cr: the strike is off." With no Governor it reads "General strike on Food: Food plots make 20% this month."
  • The TV while it waits: in planning, while the strike is unpaid, the TV's side shows the Governor's mark and "Waiting for Kai, the Governor", with "Pay the strikers 250 cr from the fund, or let them strike. After the votes."
  • Paid: the game's own news line shows in the TV news, for example "Lena pays the food strikers 250 cr."

The 250 cr is COLONY.strike.cost = 250; the 20% is COLONY.strike.output = 0.2, the same number production reads.

The riot's damage. A damaged object that has a plot, such as the park, stands on the map until the riot reaches it, then takes the map's HIT mark. The hospital has no plot, so it gets no map mark: the changes card and the log say "Hospital damaged". The build site's row then shows "Damaged in the riot", as it does today.

The gauges on the card. Any event that moves mood, prestige, air or reputation says so on its changes card, up or down, wherever the change comes from (this includes the mood parts of the immigration wave and MULE theft). A plot event that moves a gauge also gets the changes card, and its log line ends with the gauge, for example "Lena's Food farm stands idle · Mood −2".

The event show's look for all of these comes from the event gallery Heiko approved (design/mocks/ws8-events, the riot's angry mob among them).

The Happiness gauge at the month end

At the month end the TV's Happiness gauge opens on last month's number and, a moment later, counts to this month's. As it crosses a band the label flips (Restless to Unhappy, say) and the gauge pulses. It gives no reasons, only the usual "↓ n this month".

  • It plays once per month end. Someone pressing Ready does not replay it; the next month end plays again.
  • A TV that reloads or reconnects during the month end, or one with reduced motion, shows the end state at once.
  • In month 1, or when the mood did not change, nothing moves. With politics off there is no gauge.
  • Band names come from the engine's own mood bands (colony.ts::moodBand), not from a copy in the screen.

Why it is like this

Heiko asked on 28 October for the riot to be seen ("maybe an angry mob walking on the map?") and for every event to get its animation. The strike's look is WS8's option A, approved by the PM on 5 Nov: the Governor's choice sits in the phone's Now card, so the map stays in view. The screens follow the approved scaffold design/mocks/ws8-colony-events.

Warnings, forecasts, hedges and chains Backlog

The event design goes further than the game. None of this is built, and nobody is on it.

  • Warnings and forecasts. Most events announced before they hit: dust on the horizon, a pirate sighting, or an observatory's forecast. The month report already has a place for warnings, but it is always empty.
  • Hedges. Insurance, silos, vaults against pirates, levees against floods, storm shutters, firebreaks, Medicine reserves.
  • Lasting effects. A flood's fertile silt, an eruption's new mountain, a comet's falling Water price.
  • Chains. Storylines over several months: the Long Drought, Pirate Season, the Crystite Rush, the Plague Ship, the Long Winter, Colonist Unrest, the Federation Audit, the Alien Signal.
  • More events from the design catalogue: planetquake, ice melt, meteor shower, derelict ship crash, off-world recession, Crystite crash, land speculator and others.
  • Plot traits that change event risks. Bedrock, sheltered, unstable, infested and flood plain are on the map but no event reads them (see Production).
