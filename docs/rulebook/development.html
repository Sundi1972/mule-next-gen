Development

Your colonist's minute on the map: walk to the store, buy and outfit a MULE, lead it to your plot and install it, analyse ground, dodge wolves, race for glints, and cash out at the pub.

The development phase and its clock In the game

Development comes after the land grant and the land auction, and before production. Every player's colonist is on the map at the same time, steered live from their phone (or by the computer for a computer player), and everyone watches it all on the TV.

  • One clock for everyone: 60 seconds, the same for every player, every month. Hunger and sickness never shorten it; they slow your walk instead (see Pace).
  • Your turn ends when you go to the pub or when the clock runs out.
  • The phase ends early once every player is at the pub, out of time, or has dropped off the table.
  • Everything costs time: walking, standing still for an analysis, waiting at the wolves. The time you have left when you reach the pub is paid out in cash.

At the start, each colonist stands just outside one of the store's two doors, the seats taking the left and right doors in turn. A player who won a MULE at the land auction starts with it on the lead.

The game steps every colonist together in small ticks of 50 ms, on the server and in the simulator alike, so a replay of the same seed and the same moves plays the same.

Why it is like this

Heiko, 28 Oct: "if we just steer it via the walking then managing the time bars also gets easier. Then we only have one global time bar for all users, which is much better." From that the PM ruled that every per-player condition acts through walking pace, never the clock. The length, 60 seconds, is Heiko's of 4 Nov.

Walking the map In the game

You steer your colonist with the stick (a thumb pad on the phone, arrow keys on a computer) and do things with one action button. You walk freely, in any direction, diagonals included (a diagonal is no faster). At full pace on plains a colonist covers 2.5 plots a second. Other land is slower:

  • Lakes block. A lake plot is worked from its shore: stand on the land beside it, within 0.45 of a plot of its edge, and the button offers the lake plot (the words add "from the shore").
  • Other people's land may be walked across freely.
  • The store's own plot cannot be walked on; you go in through a door.
  • Leading a MULE slows you to 0.9 of your speed. The MULE walks a little behind you.

Example

Your plot is four plots east of the store across plains, then one plot of swamp. Fed and well, you walk the plains at 2.5 plots a second (1.6 s) and the swamp at 1.25 (0.8 s): about 2.4 seconds. Leading a MULE it takes 2.4 ÷ 0.9 = about 2.7 seconds.

Why it is like this

Heiko's walking rule R1 (28 Oct) set 0.4 s a plot for the first, tap-to-walk version. When steering arrived, Heiko set the steered pace to the same, 2.5 plots a second, on 4 Nov ("1.25 was half"). The land speeds are Heiko's starting values from the arcade walking design.

Pace: hunger, sickness and species In the game

Each colonist walks at a pace, a share of a fed, healthy colonist's speed. It is worked out once, when development starts, and applies on the map and in the store alike. Three things multiply together:

WhatPace factor
Fed (holding the month's Food)1
Hungryfalls in a straight line from 1 (all the Food you need) to 0.6 (none)
Sick, with no Medicine0.5
Zipwing (species perk)1.25

Hunger

The Food you need this month is 2, plus 1 for every 4 crew or part of four (a working raw plot has a crew of 1, a factory 2, a green belt none). At the start of development, if you hold less Food than that, you are hungry. The screens name how hungry by the share of Food missing:

Share of the month's Food missingShown as
nonefed
under 30%peckish
30% to under 70%hungry
70% or morestarving

The words are bands only; the pace itself slides smoothly with the Food you hold. Hunger and sickness are public: everyone can see who is hungry or sick.

Sickness

At the start of each development every player rolls for sickness: 3% at base, more in dirty air (+15% from Smoggy, +30% from Toxic, +50% in Collapse) and +3% for every air point your own plots caused last month (at most +40%; a filter halves your points), never above 90%. A rare epidemic (0.5% a month) makes everyone sick this development and the next. If you are sick and hold a Medicine, you take one and are well; otherwise you walk at half pace this turn.

Example

Brix has five working raw plots: a crew of 5, so he needs 2 + 2 = 4 Food. He holds 1: three quarters missing, "starving". His hunger factor is 1 − 0.4 × 0.75 = 0.7. He is also sick with no Medicine: 0.7 × 0.5 = 0.35. On plains he walks 2.5 × 0.35 = under 0.9 plots a second. A Zipwing in the same state would walk at 0.35 × 1.25 = about 0.44 of full pace.

Why it is like this

The slowest hunger pace, 0.6, is Heiko's of 4 Nov ("Yes, 60%"). It is the strongest slowdown with which a starving colony can still recover: at 0.2 a starving colonist could not reach Food and the pub inside the turn, and the colony went short in 58% of months; from 0.6, under 15%. Sick pace 0.5 and the sickness odds are Heiko's catalogue numbers (28 Oct). The Zipwing perk is 1.25 because at 1.5 Zipwings won 37% of four-player games instead of a fair 25%.

The store floor In the game

The store is one hall, entered by an airlock on its left or right side. Walk up to a door from outside and press ENTER STORE; inside, stand in a door and press LEAVE ("to the colony", or "with your MULE"). The hall has two rows of rooms off one walkway:

RowRooms, left to right
Back wall (outfitters)Food, Water, Energy, Smithore, Crystite, Rare earth
Front rowThe works (three bays: Alloy, Electronics, Medicine), Green belt, the corral, the pub

At full pace the walk from the left door to the corral takes 3 seconds. Hunger, sickness, the Zipwing perk and a MULE on the lead change your speed in the hall too. Standing in a room is being at it: the action button changes to what that room offers. You can also sell wolf pelts at either door (see Wolves).

Why it is like this

Heiko's store layout "One hall, two rows" (21 Oct): six outfitters along the top, the corral in the middle of the bottom row, the works and green belt to one side, the pub by the door, all on one screen.

Buying a MULE In the game

Stand in the corral, not leading a MULE, and press BUY MULE · price. You pay at once and lead an empty MULE. You can lead only one MULE at a time. If you are short of money the button is dim and says by how much ("170 cr · 30 short"); if the corral is empty it reads "NO MULES LEFT · the pub pays now".

The MULE price

A MULE costs its labour, 120 cr, plus the Smithore it is built from (1 unit at the store's posted Smithore price), times a scarcity factor:

price = (120 + Smithore price) × scarcity factor, rounded.

  • The factor starts at 1.
  • Each month the corral sells out, it rises by 15% for the next month.
  • Each month at least half of the MULEs that were in the corral when development began are left over, it falls by 10%, but never below 0.6.
  • A Gearling pays 95% of the price (rounded), for MULEs and outfits alike.

Example

Smithore is posted at 50: a MULE costs 120 + 50 = 170. Last month the corral sold out, so the factor is 1.15: 170 × 1.15 = 195.5, so 196. A Gearling pays 186.

The corral

The store starts every game with 10 MULEs. At each month's end it builds new ones, up to 2 per player plus 2 spare (10 in a four-player game, 14 with six), using 1 unit of its own Smithore for each. Short of Smithore, it builds fewer. MULEs come back to the corral when the store buys one at the land auction, when the colony wins a plot with a MULE on it, and (soon) when you end your turn still leading a swapped-out MULE (see the swap).

Installing a MULE In the game

Lead an outfitted MULE onto one of your own plots (or to the shore of your lake plot) and press INSTALL · GOOD. The MULE stays there and works that plot every month from now on, until it is taken off, sold, stolen or struck by a meteor. A plot makes nothing without a MULE.

A plot remembers how many months in a row its outfit has run (factories gain from experience). Installing a different outfit starts that count again from zero; putting the same outfit back on a plot it was taken off this turn keeps the old count.

Important for the button: while you lead an outfitted MULE and stand on your own plot, the button offers INSTALL before anything else. So analyse a plot before you bring the MULE to it.

Installing over a MULE: the swap Being built

Today

If the plot already has a MULE, the button reads SWAP and warns "your Food MULE runs away" (or "same outfit, nothing gained" if the outfits match). Pressing it puts the new MULE on the plot, and the old MULE simply vanishes. It is lost, with no refund.

What Heiko ruled

Heiko met this on 5 Nov: he led a Water MULE to his Food plot, pressed install, and the Food MULE vanished. He expected to lead the Food MULE away. His ruling, built now:

  • Installing over a plot's MULE swaps: the new MULE goes on the plot and you now lead the old one, with its outfit, as if you had taken it off.
  • If you still lead that old MULE when your turn ends, it goes back to the store's corral. You get no money for it.
  • Installing the same outfit over itself is refused: "Same outfit: nothing to swap".
  • A button to hand a MULE back to the corral mid-turn for a refund is in the backlog, for Heiko to decide.

Example

Lena's plains plot has a Food MULE. She leads in a new Energy MULE and presses SWAP. The Energy MULE now works the plains plot; Lena leads the Food MULE. She walks it to her bare river plot and installs it there: two working plots for the price of one MULE. Had she gone to the pub still leading it, it would have gone back to the corral.

The warning while you lead it Designed

While you lead a MULE you swapped off a plot, the walking screen on your phone says so: "Turn ends: this MULE goes back to the corral". Install it on a plot of yours before your turn ends, or the store takes it back for nothing.

Why it is like this

Agreed by the PM on 5 Nov, from the UX team's read of the swap rule: nothing on screen told you that the MULE you were leading would go back at turn end. Not built yet.

What is changing

Ruled by Heiko on 5 Nov; the details (corral at turn end, no refund, same outfit refused) are the PM's ruling AM-12 the same day. Being built by the Auctions team (WS3) in the game rules, with named edits by the Strategy team (WS1) for the button and the computer players; the first failing test is on branch ws3-install-swap.

Taking a MULE off and re-outfitting In the game

Stand on your own plot that has a MULE, not leading one, and the button can offer TAKE MULE: the MULE comes onto your lead and the plot stops working until a MULE is installed there again.

With a MULE you took off this turn, go to another outfitter and press RE-OUTFIT · GOOD · price. It costs the full outfit price for the new good. The outfitter for the good it already has is dim ("already fitted"), and a MULE bought this turn cannot be re-outfitted. Then install it wherever you like.

If your turn ends while you still lead a MULE you took off a plot (even one that bolted from wolves), it goes back to that plot with the outfit and run of months it had. Nothing is lost.

The button offers one thing at a time on your own plot, in this order: install (when leading an outfitted MULE), analyse (if you have not analysed the plot), fit a filter (if you hold Alloy and the plot has none), take the MULE. So on a plot you have not analysed, TAKE MULE appears only after you analyse it.

Why it is like this

Heiko, 28 Oct, approved re-outfitting a MULE at the store for the outfit price, as a rule change (AM-2). Only taken-off MULEs re-outfit (AM-14) so an outfit is chosen once at purchase. The button order (AM-9, WS6, approved by Heiko with the arcade mocks on 29 Oct) puts TAKE after ANALYSE and FIT FILTER so those two are never hidden on a plot with a MULE.

When a MULE runs off In the game

A MULE you bought (or won at the land auction) and did not install by the end of your turn runs off and is lost, with no refund. That happens when the clock runs out and when you walk into the pub leading it. The pub pays nothing back for it.

Still leading at turn endWhat happens
A MULE bought this turn, empty or outfittedRuns off, lost
A MULE won at the land auctionRuns off, lost
A MULE taken off one of your plots this turnGoes back to that plot, unchanged
A MULE you led away in a swapToday: it already vanished at the swap. Being built: back to the corral, no refund

Two other ways to lose a MULE are events: a MULE theft (a 1% chance in development and again at the month end) takes one installed MULE off one of your plots, and a meteor strike destroys the MULE on the plot it hits. See Events.

Wolves In the game

On forest, hills and tundra, a pack of 3 wolves may come for your colonist: a 10% chance each second you are on that land, and not again for 20 seconds after a pack. You have 3 seconds to choose; doing nothing is a fight.

ChoiceHow it goesWinLose
Run (push the stick)The wolves chase at 0.88 of full walking speed. They give up after 5 seconds, 4 plots, or when you reach the store.You got awayBitten: half speed until you reach the store or one of your own plots
Fight (press)Up to 10 seconds in place. A wolf crouches (0.8 s to dodge) then lunges; dodge with the stick, strike with the button at a wolf left open.3 strikes: 2 pelts, which the store buys at either door for 20 cr each3 bites: the MULE you lead bolts; with no MULE, you are stunned for 4 seconds

A bolted MULE runs 4 plots in a random direction and stands. It is still yours. Walk within 0.6 of a plot of it and press CATCH MULE; until then you cannot outfit or install it.

The glint race In the game

Now and then a Crystite sparkle lights one plot on every screen at once: "A Crystite glint at 7·3! First there wins". The first colonist to step onto that plot, or to press GRAB THE GLINT within 1.2 plots of its middle, wins 1 Crystite.

A glint can light any plot a colonist can stand on, owned or free, but never a lake, the store, or a plot someone is standing on as it lights. A player already at the pub cannot win it. Nobody there in time: "The glint faded". The prize goes straight into your stock. A computer player never presses GRAB when its press could fall through to another action: not on the tick the glint fades, and not when a rival may take it first; racing, it walks onto the glint instead (measured: no paid MULE lost to a stray assay in 960 turns).

The corral scramble In the game

When a sale leaves the corral with 2 MULEs or fewer and somebody still needs one, every screen calls a race: "2 MULEs left! Race to the corral". It is first there, first served at the normal BUY MULE. Each sale is told ("Ossa got one: 1 left"), and the last one ends it: "Brix got the last one: the corral is empty", then who went without. Otherwise it ends after 15 seconds, or once everyone who needed a MULE has one. There is at most one scramble a turn.

A player "needs a MULE" when they are not at the pub, not leading one, own a plot with no MULE, and can afford the price.

Analysing a plot In the game

Stand on any plot you have not analysed yet, owned or free, with or without a MULE, and press ANALYSE. Your colonist stands still while the button reads ASSAYING: 8 seconds, or 4 for a Mollox. Then you know the plot's rating for all six raw goods and its traits, privately and for the rest of the game. It costs no money, only time, which also lowers your pub payout. A lake plot is analysed from its shore.

Ratings, rings and what the drone does are in Land and geology.

Filters In the game

On your own plot with no filter, holding at least 1 Alloy, the button offers FIT FILTER · 1 Alloy. The Alloy comes from your own stock. A filter halves the air pollution the plot's MULE causes and stops its pollution spreading to the plots around it. It stays on the plot (a meteor strike destroys it). See Production and Colony and politics for what pollution does.

The pub In the game

Walk into the pub, in the store's front row, and press PUB · END TURN. The button shows what you will win. Your turn is over: you cannot do anything else this month. The earlier you go, and the later in the game, the more it pays:

payout = 20 + (60 + 20 × month) × (seconds left ÷ 60), rounded.

Computer players on the floor In the game

Computer players walk the same floor under the same rules: the same speeds, the same pace from hunger and sickness, the same button. They differ only in how they choose.

  • They stand 0.5 seconds at a counter before pressing, like a person reading the button.
  • They head for the pub when the clock shows no more than their walk there plus 2 seconds, and buy a MULE only if the errand, allowed 10% longer than planned, still leaves that margin.
  • At the default level (Trader) they lead at most 2 MULEs out of the store a month (Rookie 1, Tycoon 3).
  • They analyse an unknown plot of their own when its two best uses look close, at most 2 a month (Rookie 1), never with under 20 seconds left.
  • Glint: three in four race for a glint within 8 plots, only if their errand still fits afterwards. Scramble: nine in ten who need a MULE race, from within 10 plots of the store.
  • Wolves: half of them fight rather than run; they never lead a MULE across wolf land, and take wolf land on about half their other walks, so the table sees them meet packs.

They know only what a phone in their seat would show; see What the computer players know and Computer players.

The old tap walk and development screen Being built

Before steering, development worked by tapping: you tapped a destination and an action (the corral, an outfitter, a plot, the pub), and the game walked your colonist there in grid steps, never diagonally, at 0.4 seconds a plot, plus 1 second across the store floor to the corral, divided by your pace. Today every game at the table steers; the server refuses a tap move ("Development is steered: walk there and press the button"). The tap walk survives only inside the code: the old phone screen is still shown for a game started without steering, which only tests do.

All owners have approved it; it is waiting to be brought up to date with trunk and merged. Afterwards the Strategy team (WS1) and the Brain team (WS2) retire what is left behind: the tap walk's time formula, the on/off switch for steering, and the computer players' tap branches.
