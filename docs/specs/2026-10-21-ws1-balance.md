## §0 · Business Goals & Value

### G-1 · A fair, lively economy against AI players `[handoff]`

The Mission Goal, as written.

- **Who benefits** `[review-only]`: a family (or one player) at the table with strategist seats; and the programme, whose
  G-1 proof is red today.
- **Today** `[review-only]`: `pnpm sim -- --games 40 --seed 101` misses 5 of 10 checks: MULE price 90th percentile 1147
  (target under 500), corral sold out in 25% of months (under 10%), Food above 3x reference in 55% of games (33-50%),
  colonists short in 50% of months (under 15%), owned plots developed 53% (70% or more).
  `verified_by: executed` 2026-10-21 on ws1-balance @145925e. The old simple AI met the developed, corral and hunger checks
  on the same engine (`docs/technical-plan.md`, "What the simulator showed": 95% developed, corral never sold out,
  colonists short 13%), so the regression is in the strategist's play.
- **Value** `[review-only]`: AI opponents that develop their land, mine the Smithore the colony needs and keep Water on the
  shelf. A human table sees MULEs at sane prices and a colony that grows instead of starving, and G-1 can be signed off.
- **UBER-AC-1:** `pnpm sim -- --games 40 --seed 101` prints `ok` on all 10 lines. Seeds 101-140 are held out: tuning runs
  on seeds 1-40 only. The PR records both runs and the exact commands.
- **UBER-AC-2:** on the same run, the five checks that print `ok` on 101-140 today still print `ok`: MULE price median
  150-300, no AI broke for 3 months in a row, lowest store capital 500 or more, lowest colony fund 0 or more, Collapse 10%
  or less.

**Non-goals** `[review-only]`
- Changing the 10 checks or their thresholds (ruled out: they are G-1's proof, from the design doc).
- Retuning engine numbers to cover for AI play (START stock, `UPKEEP.colonistBudget`, price momentum, store reserves).
  That needs a PM ruling backed by numbers from both seed sets.
- The LLM decider and the `Decider` / `ShortOption` / `DecisionContext` shapes (WS2; frozen).
- Auctions, lots, listed plots and MULEs (WS3), and the store-buys prices in `LOTS`.
- `plotValue(obs, mind, index)` / `muleValue(obs, mind)` for WS3: owed by WS1 under SEAMS, shipped as a separate small PR
  once these changes settle.
- Win-rate fairness by persona or species (not a check; its own spec if wanted).

**Rough cost** `[review-only]`: chosen approach (B) about 3-4 PRs, roughly 2-3 days of agent time, mostly measure-and-tune
loops at about 11 s per 40-game run. Thin baseline (A) about half a day.

## §2 · The Logic `[handoff]`

```
RULE-1 · Does a seat want the lit plot                       shape: decision table
inputs
  idle       integer ≥ 0     the seat's own plots with no outfit
  perMonth   integer ≥ 1     mind.level.mulesPerMonth
  money      integer         the seat's cash
  price      integer > 0     mulePriceFor(obs, me), the MULE price this seat pays
first match wins
  #  when                                              then
  1  idle ≥ perMonth and money < (idle + 1) × price    do not want it
  2  otherwise                                         today's rule (ranking share by sweep)
examples
  idle 2, perMonth 2, money 400, price 200 -> not wanted
  idle 2, perMonth 2, money 700, price 200 -> today's rule
  idle 0, any money -> today's rule
```

```
RULE-2 · Price of units sold into a coming shortage          shape: formula (AM-1)
rollout
  the outlook's rollout of the status quo also sells, after each development, the
  MULEs the seats claim land for: corral −= min(corral, players × GAME.claimsPerGrant)
inputs
  shortFW(g)   true | false   g is Food or Water and the rollout's upkeep records a
                              colonist shortage of g (the month report's `shortages`)
  shortS       true | false   after an upkeep in the rollout, the corral holds fewer
                              MULEs than its monthly target
                              (players × claimsPerGrant + MULE.spareInCorral)
unknown
  no rollout (lookAhead false, Rookie): never short. The Rookie stays naive by design.
then
  nothing short   -> today's valuation, unchanged
  shortFW(g)      -> for months 2..horizon, units of g are priced at no less than the
                     colonists' budget ceiling, UPKEEP.colonistBudget[g] / (1 + PRICE.storeSpread)
  shortS          -> for months 2..horizon, Smithore is priced at no less than its posted
                     price at the end of a rollout extended to STRATEGIST.horizon months
boundaries
  any new factor or month count is a STRATEGIST number in config.ts (HC-7)
examples
  Water short from month 4 in the rollout -> a Water farm in month 3 is worth more than
  at today's posted 20
  Water plentiful -> the same farm's value is unchanged
```

```
RULE-3 · Reserve veto on a development candidate             shape: decision table
inputs
  cashAfter  integer         money − (MULE price + outfit cost)
  keepBack   integer ≥ 0     cashReserve(obs, mind, o, cures) as today
  covers     integer ≥ 0     credits the candidate's own first production is worth
                             toward next month's needs: for a farm of Food, Water or
                             Energy, the units that fill the seat's own shortfall at the
                             reserve's price for them; for anything else, its first
                             month's output at the outlook price, after its own Energy
first match wins
  #  when                                  then
  1  corral empty / month's MULEs used     veto, as today
  2  cashAfter < 0                         veto: it cannot pay
  3  cashAfter + covers < keepBack         veto, "under the reserve"
  4  otherwise                             no reserve veto (canFeed still applies)
invariant
  a farm whose own first output fills the shortfall the reserve was kept for is never
  vetoed by row 3
examples
  cash left 50, kept back 300, Water farm covering 4 units of a Water shortfall
  priced at 75 -> allowed
  cash left 50, kept back 300, Smithore mine whose first output sells for 120 -> vetoed
  cash left -20 -> vetoed (row 2), whatever it covers
```

```
RULE-4 · Can the seat feed a factory                          shape: rollout
inputs
  for each input g of the recipe other than Energy:
  ownFarm(g)    true | false    an own plot makes g
  stock(g)      integer ≥ 0
  need'(g)      household need with the new factory's crew included (needsOf + crew 2)
                for Food and Water; 0 otherwise
  q(g)          the recipe's input per run
  spoil(g)      GOOD_CONFIG[g].spoilage
then
  ownFarm(g) -> fed for g
  else carry = stock(g); for each of factoryInputMonths months:
         carry -= need'(g) + q(g); if carry < 0 -> not fed; carry = floor(carry × (1 − spoil))
  fed when every input is fed
examples
  month 1, no farms, 6 Food, 6 Water, Medicine works -> not fed
  own Food and Water farms, any stock -> fed
```

```
RULE-5 · MULEs counted as Smithore demand                    shape: formula
  target = players × GAME.claimsPerGrant + MULE.spareInCorral    (as runUpkeep builds)
  wanted = min(idle + (free land left ? players × claimsPerGrant : 0), target)
examples
  42 idle plots, 4 players -> wanted = target
  3 idle plots, free land left, 4 players -> 3 + 8, if under target: unchanged from today
```

`walkSeconds(state: GameState, id: string, from: DevPlace, to: DevPlace): number`, with
`DevPlace = number | 'store' | 'corral'` (a map tile's index, the store, or the corral across its floor), both

`(5 x 0.4) / 1.5` = 1.33 s.

## §7 · Risks & Spikes `[review-only]`

- **S-1 · Shortage-foresight mechanism (C2). Closed, see AM-1.** Timebox: half a day. Try (a) a rollout extended to the horizon for goods the
  outlook shows short, against (b) pricing short units at the colonists' budget ceiling. Stop condition: one of them brings
  colonists short on seeds 1-40 at least 10 points below C1+C3's row without pushing the Food spike out of 33-50%; if
  neither does, report both rows to the PM. Blocks: C2 only. The loser is not built; the winner's numbers go to the PM.
- **Interactions.** C2 and C3 both make seats build more farms; together they may overshoot Food and push the Food spike
  under 33%. Mitigation: per-commit sim rows (T-AC-7) show which change moved it; tune STRATEGIST numbers, not engine ones.
- **Hunger may stay above 15% after B.** Mitigation: the PM's rule 3, one tuning round, then a retune request with numbers
  from both seed sets.
- **Cost of a longer rollout (S-1 a).** The outlook is cached per decision; a longer rollout is several more months of the
  status quo per decision. Mitigation: measure sim wall time per row; keep it under 2x today's 11 s.
