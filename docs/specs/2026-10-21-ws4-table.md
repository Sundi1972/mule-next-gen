## §0 · Business Goals & Value  `[handoff: goal lines and proofs]` `[review-only: Who / Today / Value / cost]`

Agreed with the PM on 2026-10-21; Heiko confirmed G-3.2 (double-tap zoom) the same day.

**G-3.1 · Each player plays as the species they chose.**
- Who benefits: each human player at the kitchen table.
- Today: nobody chooses. `MuleGame.start` passes only id, name and isAi, so `createGame` falls back to
  `DEFAULT_SPECIES[i]`: seat 1 is always a Zipwing, seat 2 always a Bramblet. The design says "Each player picks a
  species" (docs/design/00-concept.md). verified_by: read.
- Value: the one up-front choice the design gives a player. A ten-second pick in the lobby turns an assigned perk
  into a play-style decision, and children get the creature they like.
- Proof **UBER-AC-1:** in `pnpm e2e`, the phone in seat 1 picks Mollox, and after Start that player's creature is a
  Mollox on the phone and on the TV.

**G-3.2 · On a sideways phone, a player can see and tap their own plots comfortably in the land grant and development.**
- Who benefits: a player on a small phone (844×390).
- Today: the map is drawn fit-to-box. A 5-6 player map is 19×11 plots and renders 560×324 px in both the land grant
  and development, so a plot is **29.5 px** wide against a 44 px minimum touch target. verified_by: executed
  (2026-10-21, the real app at 844×390, six seats).
- Value: fewer mis-taps (selecting the wrong plot before Install costs real development time), and you can read
  the terrain of your own land.
- Proof **UBER-AC-2:** on an 844×390 phone in a six-player game, a double-tap in development and in the land
  grant zooms so each of your plots is at least 44 px wide, and a second double-tap shows the whole map.

**G-3.3 · At the end, every player sees why they scored what they did and whether the colony passed, and the table
sees the ranking from across the room.**
- Who benefits: every player at game over, and the family watching the TV.
- Today: the phone shows rank and total only. A colony that ends in Collapse cuts every score by a fifth
  (`POLLUTION.collapseScore`) and nobody is told. The TV list is `text-xl`. verified_by: read.
- Value: an hour-long game ends in an answer people understand (money against goods against land and MULEs, and
  what pollution cost everyone), which is what teaches the next game.
- Proof **UBER-AC-3:** at game over each phone shows money + goods + land + MULEs (and a Collapse line when there is
  one) summing exactly to its score in the TV ranking, plus the colony verdict, and the TV shows every final score.

**G-3.4 · The host runs the lobby, a player can move their seat to another device, and everyone plays in their own
colour.** (AM-13: Heiko, after playing on the rig, 2026-10-22)
- Who benefits: the host (a stray or unwanted seat, or a room to abandon), any player who starts on one device and
  wants to continue on another, and every player who wants "their" colour.
- Today: nothing removes a seat; a room closes only after 30 idle minutes. Joining from a second device makes a
  second player; the seat token that would move the seat never leaves the first device, and when it does move the
  old screen stays connected and can still send moves. Colour is the seat's position (`slot` = index). verified_by:
  read (room.ts, net/seat.ts, client.ts, derive.ts) and Heiko's play-test on 81405f2.
- Value: a lobby a family can tidy without restarting the server; Heiko's own case, start on the Mac and continue
  on the phone as the same player; colour as a second personal choice beside species.
- Proof **UBER-AC-4:** in `pnpm e2e`, the host kicks a seat and the kicked phone lands on the start screen with a
  message; a second device opens the first player's "continue on your phone" link and plays that seat while the
  first screen steps aside; a player picks a colour, it is refused to another player, and after Start the TV and the
  map draw that player in it.

**Programme proof served:** `pnpm e2e` plays lobby → species pick → a two-month game → game-over scores, calling
WS3's auction steps through marked hooks. G-3 goes green when both lanes have landed.

**Non-goals:** traits; the TV avatar walk-on; awards; a replay; a "quick game" lobby setting; zoom on
Planning or on the TV; pinch-zoom and pan; the land and asset auction screens and the e2e auction steps (WS3);
politics screens; restyling the lobby (WS6 redesigns it; G-3.4 places its controls simply); a host kick or close
after Start; the score constants themselves (WS1, PR #11); redrawing the older upright phone mocks in
`design/mocks/holo-table`, which predate the landscape ruling.

**Rough cost:** approach A, four PRs, about three to four days of agent time. Thin-only alternative (species and e2e,
no zoom, existing game-over): about two PRs.

## §0.1 · Decision Record  `[review-only]`

- **Chosen, A (thin, four PRs):** species chips in the lobby; a double-tap or a corner button toggles the map
  between the whole colony and your own land; score breakdown and verdict on the phone, totals for 3 m on the TV;
  e2e with a test-only speed option and a CI job. Every UBER-AC is met.
- **B (A plus pinch and pan, about +1 PR):** loses because no proof needs it, and dragging the map competes with
  tapping plots on the very screen where a wrong selection costs development time.
- **C (full ceremony, 6+ PRs):** its own species screen, a TV walk-on, an animated reveal, zoom everywhere. Loses
  because everything beyond A is a non-goal.
- **Two-PR thin baseline (no zoom, existing game-over):** loses because G-3.2 and G-3.3 are agreed goals and
  the measurement (29.5 px) confirmed the zoom problem is real.

## §2 · The Logic  `[handoff]`

```
RULE-1 · Accepting a pick                                   shape: decision table

inputs   started   true | false
         sender    host | other human seat
         pick      a string from the client
         picks     the room's allow-list, or absent

first match wins
  #  when                                  then
  1  picks is absent                       refuse: "This game has no choices to make"
  2  started = true                        refuse: "The game has already started"
  3  pick not in picks                     refuse: "Unknown choice"
  4  otherwise                             set this seat's pick; broadcast

unknown   no pick on a seat means "not chosen", NOT "chosen the first species": the engine default
          (DEFAULT_SPECIES by seat) applies at Start · picks = [] (present but empty) is not ruled: today
          row 3 answers "Unknown choice" (AM-8)
boundaries any human seat may pick for itself only; the host has no say over others' picks; duplicates allowed;
          AI seats never pick
examples  host picks mollox → host seat mollox · Mia picks bramblet while Brix (AI) is bramblet → allowed ·
          pick after Start → refused · pick "dragon" → refused                                  → B-AC-1, T-AC-4
```

```
RULE-2 · Game time under phaseScale                          shape: formula

inputs   phaseScale  number in (0, 1], default 1          maxMonths  integer ≥ 1, default GAME.maxMonths
  wait(ms)       = ms × phaseScale          for PHASE_MS waits, phaseEndsAt and each development clock
  auctionDt(dt)  = min(dt, WALK.tickMs × 3) / phaseScale   (clamp first, so a stalled loop still can't teleport)
unknown   an absent option means real time; phaseScale is never 0 (rejected at construction: it would divide by 0)
examples  phaseScale 1 → today's timings exactly (T-AC-2) · 0.1 → the land grant waits 6 s; planning has no clock
          and waits for every Ready (AM-27)
```

```
RULE-3 · Score parts                                         shape: formula (existing rule, now exposed)

  raw       = money + Σ stock[g] × posted price[g] + land + muleValue       (unrounded, exactly today's sum)
  money     = round(player money)          money can carry fractions from taxed sales, so it is rounded too
  land      = plots owned × GAME.plotScore                    (integer)
  muleValue = installed MULEs × mulePrice(state)              (integer: mulePrice rounds)
  goods     = round(raw) − money − land − muleValue           (goods absorbs every rounding, so the lines add up)
  before    = round(raw)
  total     = colonyFailed ? round(raw × POLLUTION.collapseScore) : before
  cut       = before − total
unknown   none: every input exists for every player at every phase
invariant total equals today's score() for every state (HC-2); money + goods + land + muleValue = before exactly,
          and before − cut = total exactly; cut = 0 whenever the colony did not fail
examples  money 2140, goods 610, 6 plots, 5 MULEs at 150, passed → total 5300, cut 0 ·
          the same in Collapse → total 4240, cut 1060                                          → B-AC-3, T-AC-6
```

```
RULE-4 · Map zoom                                            shape: state machine + formula

states   whole ⇄ zoomed
  whole  --double-tap or button, you own ≥ 1 plot-->  zoomed
  zoomed --double-tap or button-->                    whole
  any    --you own 0 plots-->                         whole, button hidden, double-taps ignored
double-tap  two pointer-ups on the map within 300 ms and 24 px; each tap still selects as today
zoom box    your plots' bounding box plus one plot on every side, clipped to the map, grown to the map's
            shape, scale = min(3, W / boxW, H / boxH) (the 3 is MAX_ZOOM in ZoomMap.tsx, AM-12), centred
            and kept inside the map
lifetime    zoom stays on across a store trip within one development phase; it resets between the land grant
            and development
unknown   "own plots" means plots owned now; the box follows your plots live: a plot claimed while zoomed
          reframes the view
examples  6-player map, plots spanning a 4×2 block → box 6×4 → scale ≈ 2.75 → plots ≈ 81 px at 844×390 ·
          no plots → no button, double-tap does nothing                                 → B-AC-2, T-AC-8, T-AC-9
```

```
RULE-5 · Kick and close                                      shape: decision table

inputs   sender     host | other human seat (a board, having no seat, is refused before this table:
                    "Join the game first")
         started    true | false
         target     a seat id (kick only)

kick: first match wins
  #  when                                  then
  1  sender is not the host                refuse: "Only the host can do that"
  2  started = true                        refuse: "The game has already started"
  3  target is no seat at this table       refuse: "No such seat"
  4  target is the host's own seat         refuse: "Close the table instead"
  5  otherwise                             remove the seat (its token dies with it); if a human was connected,
                                           send it { removed, 'kicked' } and detach it; broadcast
close: first match wins
  1  sender is not the host                refuse: "Only the host can do that"
  2  started = true                        refuse: "The game has already started"
  3  otherwise                             send { removed, 'closed' } to every connected seat and board, detach
                                           them, dispose the room and drop it from the registry
leave (AM-15): first match wins. Before the table (AM-18): a screen whose seat another device has since taken
  (it no longer holds the seat) lets go of that screen only: no refusal, no seat changes, its later messages
  are refused ("Join the game first"). The rows below apply to the screen that holds the seat
  1  sender is the host and not started    refuse: "Close the table instead"
  2  started = true                        detach this connection only; the seat stays (today's behaviour: away)
  3  otherwise                             remove the sender's own seat (its token dies with it); broadcast

confirm (client, AM-17): kick and close are sent only after the host confirms in `host-dialog` (`kick-confirm`,
          `close-confirm`); cancel sends nothing. Leave needs no confirmation
unknown   a kicked player who joins again by code gets a new seat, like any newcomer
boundaries seat ids are never reused; a kicked seat's colour and pick are freed; AI seats can be kicked
examples  host kicks Brix (AI) → 3 seats left · Mia kicks Heiko → refused · host closes → every phone on the start
          screen with "The host closed the table", the TV on the room picker              → B-AC-6, T-AC-10
```

```
RULE-6 · Moving a seat to another device                     shape: decision table

on { t: 'handoff' }: first match wins (AM-16)
  #  when                                  then
  1  the sender holds no seat              refuse: "Join the game first" (a board, or a detached screen)
  2  otherwise                             issue a new code for the sender's seat (any earlier unused code for that
                                           seat dies), expiring 2 minutes from now(); send { handoffCode, code,
                                           expiresAt } to the sender only
on join with a handoff code: first match wins
  1  no live code matches (unknown, used,  refuse: "That link has expired"; no seat changes
     or past expiresAt)
  2  otherwise                             the code dies; if the seat has a live connection, send it { removed,
                                           'moved' } and detach it; take the seat; issue the seat a NEW token and
                                           send it in welcome
on join with a seat token: today's rejoin, unchanged (the device's own token, never carried in a link)
on load with ?room=CODE&handoff=CODE (client)
  remove `handoff` from the address bar, join with it; welcome's token is saved as this device's seat
on { removed, reason } (client)
  forget the saved seat, stop reconnecting, show the start screen and the reason:
  kicked → "The host removed you from the table" · closed → "The host closed the table" ·
  moved → "You're playing on another device now"

unknown   a stale handoff code → "That link has expired"; a stale or rotated seat token after Start → today's
          refusal "The game has already started"; in the lobby → a new seat (today's behaviour)
game (AM-19) when a live old screen is replaced, the room calls Game.seatMoved(seatId) (CT-8): the game drops any
          input the old screen was holding. If the old device had already dropped, the room calls
          connectionChanged(seatId, true) instead, as a token rejoin does
boundaries works in the lobby and after Start; the seat id is the same; a board never holds a seat token or a
          handoff code; anything that removes the seat (kick, a lobby leave, close) kills its code with it; a join
          carrying both a handoff code and a seat token uses the code; a non-string `handoff` is "Bad message"
known limit (AM-24) if the new device's connection drops after the room has spent the code but before its
          `welcome` arrives, its reconnect re-sends the spent code and is refused. The old device has already stepped
          aside, so the seat is left with a token nobody holds, and after Start that player can't get back in. The
          window is one network round trip; accepted (PM ruling, 2026-10-28). A spent code that could reclaim its
          seat was rejected: it would widen the takeover window. A two-phase confirm is the fix if players hit it
examples  Heiko's Mac opens "Move to your phone" → his phone scans the QR → the phone is Heiko, the Mac shows
          "You're playing on another device now" · the same QR scanned again → "That link has expired" · a
          code scanned after 2 minutes → "That link has expired"                                       → B-AC-7, T-AC-11
```

```
RULE-7 · Colours                                             shape: decision table + invariant

palette   colour indexes 0..5 = SLOT_COLOR p1..p6 (light and dark themed)
on a new seat (human or AI)      the lowest free colour index
on { t: 'color'; color } from a human seat: first match wins
  #  when                                  then
  1  started = true                        refuse: "The game has already started"
  2  color outside the palette             refuse: "Unknown colour"
  3  another human seat holds it           refuse: "That colour is taken"
  4  an AI seat holds it                   the AI moves to the lowest free colour; this seat takes it; broadcast
  5  otherwise                             this seat takes it; broadcast
invariant no two seats share a colour, at any time
unknown   nobody picks → seat i keeps colour i, exactly today's drawing
boundaries a kick frees the colour; the game uses the colour at Start for the whole game; in row 4 the picking seat
          gives up its old colour first, so "free" includes it (the only reading that works at a full table); the
          palette is 0..maxSeats−1, so a game with a fixed palette keeps maxSeats within it (6 for M.U.L.E.)
examples  Mia picks red (an AI's) → the AI moves to the lowest free colour · Jonas then picks red → refused
                                                                                             → B-AC-8, T-AC-12, T-AC-13
```

## §4 · Failure & Ops  `[handoff]`

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| A dependency unavailable? | Not applicable to the game: everything is in-process. For the e2e, a missing Chromium fails fast with Playwright's own message (C2). CI installs its own (C4). |
| Retry / poison / page? | A refused pick is a user error (TableError), shown as a toast; nothing retries. |
| Idempotency? | A pick is idempotent: the same pick twice leaves the same state. A pick races Start only through the room's single thread: whichever arrives first wins by RULE-1. |
| What can race? | The host pressing Start while another phone picks. The room handles messages one at a time, so the game starts with the picks received before Start. |
| What still works if broken? | If the pick fails, everyone plays their seat's default species, which is exactly today's behaviour. |
| How do we know it works? | `pnpm e2e` in CI on every PR (C4). |
| G-3.4: what can race? | Kick against that phone's own pick or a join: the room handles one message at a time, so whichever lands first wins and the later one meets RULE-5/7. A device move against the old device's move: once the room has detached the old connection, its messages are refused ("Join the game first"). |
| G-3.4: security? | The seat token is a bearer secret and never leaves its device (AM-16). A move uses a separate handoff code: bound to one seat, single use, dead after 2 minutes or when a newer one is issued, stripped from the address bar on load; the seat gets a fresh token on the move. A photographed QR can't take the seat later. A family-table trust level, not an account system. |

## §9 · Ownership Boundaries  `[handoff]`

### Ownership: the score rule
- Sole owner: the new `scoreParts` in packages/engine/src/game.ts (C8); score() is a view of it.
- Read contract: `MuleView.scores[i].parts` on clients; scoreParts / score in server and AI code.
- Write contract: none; it is a pure function.
- Enforcement: the property test T-AC-6, plus review: no client code computes score parts (no prices reach the client).

### Ownership: the lobby pick
- Sole owner: `packages/tabletop/src/room.ts::Room` (accepts and stores the pick); the engine owns the default.
- Read contract: `SeatView.pick`, and `SeatInfo.pick` at Game.start.
- Write contract: the `pick` envelope only.
- Enforcement: the tabletop package boundary (the game cannot write seats) and T-AC-4.

### Ownership: the seat (G-3.4)
- Sole owner: the tabletop Room (packages/tabletop): seat lifetime (join, kick, close), its token and its colour.
  Games read `color` from the seats they are started with and never assign one.
- Enforcement: the package boundary (the game cannot write seats) and T-AC-10..12.
