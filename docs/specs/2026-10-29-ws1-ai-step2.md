## §0 · Business Goals & Value

**G-1 · Every computer player keeps a game plan across months and plays to win with it.**
- Who benefits: the players at the table, Heiko's playtest group first. Each AI should feel like a real opponent, and four AIs should play four different games.
- Today: the AI decides one move at a time, with at most two months of look-ahead. It never plans what to produce over a game, and it corners a market only by accident. (verified_by: read. This is the design doc's reading of the code on 28 Oct, and still true on trunk 6d30b69.)
- Value: an opponent worth beating. Each personality leans to its own plan (builder, specialist, monopolist, land baron, politician, spoiler), keeps it, and re-plans only when something big happens. This is what Heiko asked for ("the AI plays to win with a plan", AM-17). Local fixes can't give it.
- Proof, UBER-AC-1:
  - Run 120 sim games, 40 on each of seeds 1, 101 and 201, at Trader level. Each table has two step-2 seats and two of today's seats, the pairing rotated through the personas. The step-2 seats win at least 60% of games, against a fair share of 50%.
  - No balance check that passes on the base fails with step 2.
  - Trader-level step-2 seats attempt a monopoly in some games, and Rookie seats never do.

**G-2 · A Smart AI's strategy from Claude shows in every phase it plays, and play never waits for Claude.**
- Who benefits: the players at a table with Smart AIs. They meet an opponent whose plan is Claude's, carried out at full speed. Heiko also benefits: the $1 game budget buys strategy that reaches the board, not only chat.
- Today:
  - In the timed phases (land grant, development, the goods auction), Claude can't steer anything. It picks an option letter at a few decision points, and the strategist plays everything else from its own one-move scores.
  - There is no record of a plan for a replay to follow. (verified_by: read: `BRAIN` holds only the move path; spec A adds the writer.)
- Value: the coach-and-players split from Heiko's "Two layers". Claude writes the playbook while there is time, and the engine plays it move by move, in milliseconds, falling back on its own judgement when surprised. Without a key, the engine writes its own simpler playbook, so the game always works and the sim tests every part.
- Proof, UBER-AC-2:
  - Given a playbook that differs from the engine's own draft (another money-maker, another auction role for a good, another development route), the seat's moves in that phase follow it, or the outcome record names the fallback and why. With no playbook from Claude, it plays the engine's draft.
  - With the model stubbed to answer only after 30 s, every timed phase ends at the same game time as the same seed played keyless.

**G-3 · The AI knows every move a player can make, selling included.**
- Who benefits: the players. An AI short of cash sells a plot or a MULE as a human would, instead of going hungry. Also WS2, whose manual and tools read the list; with one list, anything Claude plans, the hands can carry out.
- Today: the phone can list and unlist a plot or a MULE for the land auction, but the AI has no such move. `drone`, `ready` and `continue` aren't AI moves either, and nothing describes the 13 moves the AI does have. (verified_by: read: `packages/ai/src/actions.ts::AiAction` against `packages/protocol` on trunk 6d30b69.)
- Value: the AI can't fall behind the rules (Heiko, 28 Oct, "What the AI knows"; AM-20 "we do b for now"). A new phone move is flagged the day it lands, not found in a playtest.
- Proof, UBER-AC-3:
  - A tier-1 test fails when any phone command in `packages/protocol` has neither an AI move nor a place on a short reviewed exclusion list (chat, emotes, lobby).
  - In UBER-AC-1's run, an AI short of cash puts a plot or a MULE up for auction in some games.
- **A wording point for Heiko.** His doc puts "when it's smart" in the one list. WS1 and WS2 propose to keep it beside the list instead: in WS2's manual, as an advice table. Each advice line names a move in the list and the playbook field that acts on it, and a WS2 test fails if either is missing. The reason: when a move pays is the strategist's valuation and the playbook's job, and a second copy inside the move list would drift from both.

**Non-goals** (each its own spec or step):
- Land picks: step 1, WS3's land module. Step 2 reads its playbook part, and WS1 reviews it.
- Market play: step 3. Buying out the store, holding back, the export ship and defending against corners are carried in the playbook's types now, but run in step 3.
- Claude writing and evaluating the playbook: WS2's spec A.
- The game record and its replay: WS3.
- Tycoon: predicting rivals, deals and bets.
- Diplomacy: whispers, offers, trust and warmth.
- Land-auction play beyond listing: playbook v1 has no land-auction play.
- Memory across games.
- The MCP server: parked.

**Rough cost:**
- Chosen (the full step 2): the engine's playbook draft (six plan kinds, re-plan triggers, persona leanings), the validator (bounds, and the level bound: no monopoly while `corners` is false), the execution layer reading the playbook for development and the goods auction, the PhaseOutcome builders for those two phases, and the move list with list, unlist and the coverage test. About four PRs, 4–6 days through subagents.
- Thin baseline: the execution layer reads only the fields the strategist already has a twin for (cash floor, money-maker, monopoly target, ranked plots, auction role, walk-away, hold, if-sold-out), and the engine's draft is today's decisions reshaped as a playbook. No plan kinds and no re-planning. One or two PRs, 1–2 days. It meets G-2's mechanics, but not G-1: today's decisions reshaped are still one move at a time.

## §0.1 · Decision Record `[review-only]`

**Chosen: C, the plan. 4 PRs, 5½–7 days, including the ½-day spike.**
- The engine drafts a complete playbook from the strategist's own valuations. One validator gates every playbook,
  including the engine's own draft.
- The seat keeps a plan across months, of one of six kinds that its persona leans to. It re-plans only on five named
  triggers.
- The development and auction hands read every field of their part. The move list gains drone, list and unlist, and a
  coverage test guards it.
- The four PRs are, in order: validator and draft, moves, hands, plan. WS2 is unblocked in that order, and the spike
  gates the plan PR.

**Thin baseline, A: today's decisions reshaped. 2 PRs, 2½–3 days** (F-3).
- **What it is:** the draft is today's `landDecision`, `goodPlan` and `tradeDecision` picks put in playbook form. The
  hands read only the fields with a twin in the strategist: cash floor, role, hold and walk-away.
- **Why it loses:** a playbook seat picks exactly what a control seat of the same persona picks on the same state. In
  UBER-AC-1's mixed tables it therefore wins its fair 50%, plus whatever selling adds, against a 60% bar.
- **It also misses UBER-AC-2's route clause.** `packages/ai/src/hands/develop.ts::DevelopHand` asks `developDecision`
  for one MULE at a time, in its `'decide'` stage, so there is no route for an edit to change.
- **How to check:** run the pairing sim on it, and read `hands/develop.ts`.

**Middle, B: the playbook plays, without a plan. 3 PRs, 4–4½ days** (C's PR-1 to PR-3).
- **What it is:** every development and auction field plays, and PhaseOutcome is built.
- **Why it loses:**
  - `Strategy.kind` stays a label that nothing reads, and the strategy is re-drafted every month from that month's
    signals. G-1's "keeps it, and re-plans only when something big happens" has no mechanism.
  - WS2's spec A PR-4 consumes "WS1 re-plan triggers" (its plan, the PR-4 row), and B builds none.
- **Choosing C costs B nothing:** B is C's first three PRs, so no WS2 unblock comes later under C.

## §1 · Solution Design `[handoff]`

**The shape.** One new pure module, `packages/ai/src/playbook/` (validate, draft, plan, replan, outcome), sits between the
strategist and the hands.
- **The strategist** still does every valuation.
- **The hands** stop asking for one decision at a time and read a playbook part instead.
- **A seat** drafts its own playbook at three fixed points a month: planning, its first development tick and its first
  auction tick. It passes the draft through the validator, writes what it plays to the game record, and plays it in
  milliseconds. From PR-4 it also keeps its plan across months until one of five named triggers fires.
- **WS2's writer** edits the same draft through the same validator.
- **The land grant** is still played by today's land hand until WS3's step-1 module reads `landGrant`. The draft records
  the plan that hand plays.

## §4 · Failure & Ops `[handoff]`

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| A dependency unavailable? | Nothing external. The draft and the validator run in-process; Claude is WS2's. A late or missing reply leaves the seat's own path in effect (RULE-6, spec A RULE-A1). (C1, C4) |
| Retry, poison, page? | A refused planning act is never retried (RULE-13). A refused development step drops its group (RULE-9 row 6). A refused edit stays refused (RULE-1). If a draft or the validator throws, that phase falls back to today's path and the seat logs it (HC-9). Nothing pages. (C1, C4, C6) |
| Idempotency, delivery? | One draft per seat per draft point per month (its first tick). draft() called by WS2 changes nothing. Adopting the same playbook twice changes nothing. A replay writes the same playbooks at the same steps (HC-3). (C4) |
| What can race? | WS2's adopt against the phase start: the phase's first tick decides. Adopted before it, the adopted part plays; after it, the seat's own path plays that month (WS2's RULE-A1 owns lateness). Two seats listing into the last lot slot: lots.ts's own order decides (LOTS.maxLotsPerMonth), and the loser's refusal is logged. (C4, C10) |
| What still works when this breaks? | `AI_PLANS.on = false` (or `--plays today`) puts every seat back on today's path, act for act (T-AC-13). A throwing draft costs that seat one phase of today's play, never the game. (C4) |
| How does an operator know it works, and that it is in use? | Every playbook played is in the game record, with `by`, `edits` and `rejected`. The sim's report adds wins by seat kind, monopoly plans, AI listings and route fallbacks. The seat's re-plan log names each trigger. (C4, C14) |

## §5 · Delivery `[handoff]`

**Code-only. There is no stored state to reach.** Games live in memory. Records made before step 2 do not replay,
because AI_PLANS changes the config fingerprint: the record's own rule (`replay.ts`, RecordMismatch).

| PR | Changes | It unblocks | Its gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| PR-1, the contract | C1, C2, C3 (bounds and leanings), C4 (draft, adopt, validator, the planning draft point, onPlaybook, table lines), C15 (onPlaybook) | WS2 spec A PR-1, Tasks 1.4b and 1.5 | T-AC-28 on trunk first (§7 R-6) |
| PR-2, the moves | C9, C10, C11, C4 (the planning part), C15 (act), C3 (sell) | WS2's PR-2 (its coverage gate) and PR-3 | — |
| PR-3, the hands | C5, C6, C7, C8, C14, C4 (the development and auction draft points) | WS2's PR-4 (outcomes) and PR-5 (C7) | merged before arcade PR-4 |
| PR-4, the plan | C12, C13, C3 (kinds, replan, monopoly), the UBER-AC-1 run | WS2's PR-4 (its re-plan row) | S-1 at 55% or better |

**A draft point switches on together with the hand that plays its part.**
- The planning point arrives in PR-1. Its land part records the land hand's own plan.
- The development and auction points arrive in PR-3.

So the record never holds a part that nothing played.

**Rollback:** set `AI_PLANS.on = false`, one line. **Point of no return:** WS2's PR-5 removes today's asks for Smart
seats. After it, the flag reverts only Classic seats.

## §7 · Risks & Spikes `[review-only]`

- **S-1 · The edge (timebox ½ day; it blocks PR-4: C12, C13).** The question: can committed plan kinds beat today's seats
  60% of the time at Trader without failing a check the base passes? A playbook seat values everything exactly as today,
  so its edge can only come from commitment.
  - *Method:* on a scratch branch off PR-3's head, add the kinds as AI_PLANS.kinds multipliers, the money-maker focus and
    the kind pick, with no triggers. Run `pnpm sim -- --plays pair --games 40` on seeds 1, 101 and 201 at Trader, and
    sweep two or three values of `AI_PLANS.moneyMaker.weight` and `kinds.monopolist.corner` by `--set`. Record the win
    share per seed and the checks the base passes.
  - *Stop:*
    - 55% or better: build PR-4 as specified.
    - Under 52%: the 60% bar goes back to Heiko, through the PM, before PR-4.
    - From 52% up to 55%: the lead decides whether one more ½ day on the levers is worth it, and tells the PM.
  - The scratch branch is never merged.
- **R-1 · Balance: a monopolist sitting on Food or Water.** A Trader monopolist that corners Food holds it back from the
  colonists, and the "Colonists short of Food or Water" check is the one at risk. #180 fixed the base; before that the
  check missed in 4 of 6 cells (AM-19 b). *Mitigation:*
  - re-measure the base on trunk at PR-4's base: seeds 1, 101 and 201, politics on and off, 40 games each. B-AC-2 then
    guards the check wherever the base passes it.
  - Water is not in `AI_PLANS.monopoly.goods`. Food stays in, because the design doc names it.
  - If B-AC-2 fails on this check, the lever is that config list, and Heiko rules whether Food stays.
  - goodPlan's existing guard still caps the value of Food and Water held when the store's shelf can't feed the colony
    (`packages/ai/src/strategist.ts::goodPlan`).
- **R-2 · Two kinds act mostly outside step 2.**
  - Land baron (the aggressive persona's favourite) acts in the land grant (WS3's step 1) and the land auction (a
    non-goal).
  - Politician acts in the elections (`Playbook.politics`: B-4, WS7's VoteHand).
  - In step 2 they differ from builder only through their multipliers in development and the auction. B-AC-5 still sees
    four kinds. *Mitigation:* the spec says so (RULE-3, RULE-14); the levers arrive with WS3's step 1, WS2's B-4 and WS7.
- **R-3 · The land part is advisory until WS3's step 1.** The recorded landGrant is the plan today's land hand plays
  (RULE-5), so the record matches play. Claude's ranked edit takes effect only once WS3's module reads it (WS2's
  B-AC-A1 waits on that, not on this spec).

  *Mitigation:* RULE-10 recomputes per-unit values, with the corner and spite premiums, at each good's status screen,
  inside the part's bounds.
