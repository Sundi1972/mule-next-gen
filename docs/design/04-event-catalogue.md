# Event catalogue

The successor has about 40 events instead of the original's dozen, and each game draws only part of them, so no two games feel alike. Most events can be seen coming and hedged against, and many leave a lasting mark on the map or the market.

## How events work

- **Event deck.** Each game shuffles the catalogue and draws about 25 events into that game's deck, weighted by difficulty. A game won't see every event, and the same event can't hit twice in a row.
- **Two kinds of effect.** Every event has a short-term effect (this month) and many have a long-term one: a changed tile, a price trend, a new building, or a follow-up event.
- **Warnings.** Most events are announced before they hit. Some show signs a month ahead, like dust on the horizon or a pirate sighting. The rest can only be forecast by an observatory (see below). A few events, like meteor strikes and quakes, always come without warning.
- **Who gets hit.** Colony events hit everyone. Local events hit one area of the map, so where you build matters. Personal events still favour the player in last place, as in the original.
- **Severity scales with the colony.** A well-supplied colony shrugs off a disaster that would break a starving one, because shortages make every event worse.
- **Chains.** Some events start a storyline that runs for several months and can be steered by what players do (see Event chains).

## Hedging and defence

Every protection costs something now, or gives up an advantage, so playing it safe is a real choice rather than a free one.

| Tool | Protects against | Cost | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Insurance (Weather, Space or Market policy) | Pays 75% of the loss in its category | Monthly premium from the store | Premium rises after each claim and before forecast disasters |
| Silo | Food and Water shortfalls; spoilage halved | Alloy to build, one plot slot | That plot produces nothing else |
| Vault | Pirate raids and theft of stored goods | Alloy and Electronics | Pirates target the richest unprotected stock |
| Levee | Floods on river plots | Alloy | Also blocks the fertile silt bonus after a flood |
| Quake bracing | Planetquakes destroying a MULE | Alloy | Small cost on each braced plot |
| Storm shutters | Dust storms on Energy plots | Alloy | Small cost on each shuttered plot |
| Firebreak | Fire and pests spreading between plots | Leave a plot empty or install a fire station | Breaks your adjacency cluster |
| Spread-out land | Local events (floods, eruptions, pests) | Nothing up front | Fewer adjacency bonuses |
| Supply contract | Price spikes and shortages | Locks a fixed price | You miss out if the price drops |
| Forward sale | Price crashes on goods you produce | Locks a fixed sale price | You miss out if the price rises |
| Medicine reserve | Disease and epidemic events | Medicine kept in stock | It spoils slowly and ties up money |
| Observatory | Gives a 70% accurate forecast of next month's events | A building on your plot; or the colony builds one by council vote | A private owner can sell forecasts, or keep them secret |
| Mutual aid pact | Food and Energy shortfalls, covered by your partner at a set price | Reputation if you fail to help | Ties you to that player's bad luck |
| Colony emergency fund | Colony-wide disasters | A tax voted by the council | Everyone pays, including players who are already well protected |

## Nature and weather

| Event | Short term | Long term | Warning | Best hedge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dust storm | Energy plots produce half | None | 1 month (visible) | Storm shutters, Energy stock |
| Drought | Food and Water -1 per plot for 2 to 3 months | River plots lose irrigation until the rains | Observatory | Silo, Water contract, ice or lake plots |
| Flood season | River plots produce no Food this month | River plots get +1 Food for 3 months (fertile silt) | Observatory | Levee, or plots off the river |
| Planetquake | Destroys one MULE on a mountain plot | Exposes new veins: +1 Smithore nearby for good | None | Quake bracing, insurance |
| Pest swarm | One player's Food plots lose half | Spreads to adjacent Food plots next month unless treated with Medicine | 1 month (visible) | Medicine, firebreaks |
| Wildfire | Burns one plot's output | Spreads along clusters of Food plots | None | Firebreak, fire station |
| Cold snap | Energy upkeep doubles this month | None | Observatory | Energy stock |
| Volcanic eruption | Ash: nearby plots -1 for 2 months | A new mountain tile appears; ash later makes soil +1 Food | Observatory, 2 months | Spread-out land, insurance |
| Ice melt | Water plots +1 this month | A new lake tile appears for the next land grant | Observatory | None needed |
| Acid rain | Energy and Food plots -1 for one month | Unshielded MULEs need repair (Alloy) | Observatory | Storm shutters, Alloy stock |

## Economy and market

| Event | Short term | Long term | Warning | Best hedge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Federation demand spike | Export price of one good doubles | Lasts 3 months, then drops back | Announced 1 month ahead | Build up stock of that good |
| Off-world recession | Export prices -40% | Lasts 4 months; the store buys less | Observatory (news feed) | Forward sales, sell for local use |
| Crystite crash | Crystite price halves | Recovers slowly over 6 months | Rumours 1 month ahead | Forward sale, spread across goods |
| New mining colony nearby | Smithore price -20% | Permanent | 2 months | Turn Smithore into Alloy instead of selling it |
| Store fire | The store loses its stock of one good | Price spikes until restocked | None | Your own stock |
| Inflation | Store prices +10% | Permanent; cash loses value | Observatory (news feed) | Hold land and goods instead of cash |
| Land speculator | Offers 50% above base for any plot this month | Land prices rise each month until the bubble bursts and they fall by half | None | Sell before the burst |
| Tech breakthrough | A new MULE upgrade becomes available | Electronics demand rises for good | 1 month | Electronics stock |
| Investor visit | Invests in the player with the best colony record | Pays a share of profits each month | None | Good reputation |

## Personal

Personal events hit one player and keep the original's spirit: good ones go more often to the player in last place, bad ones to the leader.

| Event | Effect | Lasting? | Best hedge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Package from home | +3 Food | No | Not needed |
| Inheritance | Cash, bigger for the trailing player | No | Not needed |
| Lucky prospect | One of your plots reveals Crystite | Yes | Not needed |
| Visiting mechanic | One MULE gets a free upgrade | Yes | Not needed |
| Runaway MULE | One MULE escapes; catch it during development or lose it | Possibly | Engineer trait, spare time |
| Rats in the silo | Lose half your stored Food | No | Silo upgrade |
| Crooked accountant | Lose 10% of your cash | No | Hold assets instead of cash |
| Boundary dispute | Pay a fee, or lose your smallest plot to the colony | Possibly | Good reputation lowers the fee |
| Old rival | An AI player turns hostile and rejects deals for 3 months | Yes | Reputation, pacts |

## Event chains

A chain is a storyline that unfolds over several months. Each step gives players a chance to react, so the outcome depends on what they did.

The colony can blunt each chain together: when the first warning comes, the Governor can use tax money to build a matching colony project, such as a water reservoir for the drought or a defence battery for the pirates. See Colony politics.

**The Long Drought**

1. Dry winds: the observatory warns that a drought is likely.
2. Drought for 2 to 3 months. Water prices climb.
3. Wildfires break out in large clusters of Food plots.
4. The rains return with a flood season. River plots that survived get the fertile silt bonus.

**Pirate Season**

1. A pirate ship is sighted.
2. Raids every month on the richest unprotected stock.
3. The colony council votes on a defence fund. If it passes, the raids stop. If not, they continue and grow.

**The Crystite Rush**

1. The Federation doubles the export price for Crystite.
2. A land speculator arrives, and land prices rise every month.
3. The bubble bursts: Crystite crashes and land prices halve. Players who sold at the top win big.

**The Plague Ship**

1. A derelict ship crashes. Its salvage is valuable, but its cargo is infected.
2. An epidemic follows unless the colony together holds enough Medicine.
3. If the epidemic spreads, immigration stops and demand falls for the rest of the game.

**The Long Winter**

1. The observatory sees the planet's orbit swinging wide, and a cold season is coming.
2. Cold snaps for 3 months. Energy upkeep doubles and Food plots produce 1 less.
3. The power grid fails under the load. Plots without their owner's own Energy stop working.
4. Spring thaw: melting ice creates new lake tiles for the next land grant.

Colony counter: emergency generators. Players' own counter: battery banks and an Energy stockpile.

**Colonist Unrest**

1. Food has been expensive for 2 months, and the colonists start to grumble.
2. Protests: every plot produces 1 less while Food stays above 1.5 times its reference price.
3. If prices are still high, a riot loots the colony store and destroys a third of its stock.
4. Afterwards, colonists leave and demand falls, or, if the colony fed them, immigration picks up.

Colony counter: release the grain reserve, or pass a Food price cap. This chain is set off by speculation, so Food speculators are betting against the colony.

**The Federation Audit**

1. The Federation announces an audit in 2 months, with an output target for the colony.
2. The colony has 3 months to hit the target together.
3. If it succeeds, a big grant goes to the colony fund, and a spaceport pad appears on the map. If it fails, the Federation runs the store for 3 months and all its prices rise 20%.

Colony counter: cooperation. The Governor can tie a newcomer subsidy or tax cut to the target.

**The Alien Signal**

1. A signal is picked up from somewhere in the unexplored edge of the map.
2. A race during development: the first colonist to deep-survey the source finds it.
3. The finder discovers either a super tile, or a nest of creatures that roam and damage plots each month until hunted down.

Colony counter: the colony observatory pinpoints the source, so everyone starts the race equal.
