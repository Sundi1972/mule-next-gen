# Colony politics

The colony governs itself. Players pass laws that change the rules for everyone, elect officials with real powers, and earn titles that add influence. Political power is another way to win, alongside land and money.

## How the colony is governed, in one page

- **Elections every 3 months.** The colony elects a Governor and a Sheriff in months 1, 4, 7 and 10, so a 12-month game has four terms and every player gets a real chance at power.
- **The Governor runs the money.** Each month they set the sales tax and split the colony fund across five budget lines (below). That needs no vote; it is the power of the office.
- **The colony makes the rules.** New rules only come from a fixed list of laws. The Governor proposes one law a month and everyone votes on it.
- **The colony can push back.** Once a month any player can call an overrule vote against the Governor's tax or budget. A majority of all votes undoes it and the previous month's settings stay. Twice overruled in one term means a snap election next month.
- **Everything is public.** The budget, the tax rate, every vote and who cast it show on the big screen, so the table can argue about it.

## Elections

- **When.** In the planning phase of months 1, 4, 7 and 10, before anything else happens that month. A snap election can add one more.
- **Who can run.** Any player, human or AI, declares during the planning phase. Nobody can hold Governor and Sheriff at the same time, and nobody can be Governor for more than two terms in a row.
- **How.** Secret ballot. Everyone votes with their full vote weight (land, reputation, titles, taxes paid, borrowed votes). The most votes wins; the Land Baron breaks a tie. If nobody runs, the richest player is appointed.
- **Campaigns.** Candidates make promises in chat ("no land tax", "cleanup squad from month 5"). A promise can be written as a deal card. Breaking a promise made on a deal card costs reputation, so it costs votes next time.
- **Pay.** The Governor gets 50 credits a month from the fund and the Sheriff 30. Each term served adds to the final score.

## What the Governor can do

| Power | Limits | Can the colony stop it? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set the sales tax | 0 to 15%, at most 5 points up or down per month | Yes, by overrule vote |
| Split the budget across the five lines | Only money that is in the fund; no debt | Yes, by overrule vote |
| Propose one law or repeal per month | From the fixed list of laws | The colony votes on it |
| Start a colony project | Paid from the Projects line | Yes, by overrule vote |
| Break ties in law votes |  | No |
| Order an emergency shipment of a short good | Paid from the Reserve line | No |

The Governor cannot set the other taxes (export, land, windfall, pollution). Those are laws and need a vote.

## The council

- **The Governor proposes, the colony votes.** In every planning phase the Governor can put one law, or the repeal of a law, to the vote. Players who want a law have to convince the Governor, or vote them out at the next election.
- **A simple majority of votes passes it.** The Governor breaks ties.
- **At most three laws** can be in force at once. Passing a fourth means the Governor picks which old law it replaces.

**How you get more votes.** Everyone starts with 1 vote. Influence can be earned in five ways, and the council screen shows everyone's current votes:

| Source | Extra votes | Why it makes sense |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Land | +1 per 10 plots owned | Landowners have the biggest stake in the colony |
| Reputation | +1 at 70 or above, -1 below 30 | People listen to players who keep their word |
| Land Baron title | +1 | Goes to the biggest landowner |
| Taxes and donations paid into the colony fund | +1 for every 300 credits paid during the game, up to +2 | Taxpayers get a say, so paying taxes isn't only a cost |
| Borrowed votes | Another player's votes for one month | Agreed with a deal card, so votes can be traded, rented or bought |

## Taxes and the budget

**Money in.** The colony fund is filled by the sales tax (set by the Governor), the export, land, windfall and pollution taxes when those laws are in force, the Sheriff's fines and players' donations. Who pays each tax is in the Economy model tab. The store trades with its own capital, never with the colony fund. Paying in earns extra votes, as above.

**Money out: five budget lines.** In each planning phase the Governor splits the money available that month across five lines, with sliders on their phone. Every line shows what it buys before it is confirmed, for example "Environment 200: air −8, cleans 2 plots". Money not spent stays in the fund.

| Budget line | What it pays for | Effect of each 100 credits a month (placeholders) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reserve | Emergency imports and the grain reserve | Pays for an emergency shipment of a good under half a month of cover (3 times the off-world price, at most 2 units per player), or tops up the store's capital |
| Security | The colony militia | Sabotage detection +10%; at 200 or more, pirate raids are blocked |
| Environment | Cleanup squads | Air gauge −4 and the most polluted plot cleaned by 1 level |
| Welfare | Help for the last-placed player | 100 credits to them, or a free MULE outfit |
| Projects | Building and running colony projects | Saved up until a project can be built; pays project upkeep first |

**Running out.** If the fund can't pay everything, every line is cut by the same share. Projects whose upkeep isn't paid stop working until it is.

## Colony projects

Colony projects are paid from the Projects line of the budget and protect everyone from events. Most event chains start with a warning, and that warning is the moment to build. A project takes one month to build, so it has to be started in time.

| Project | Cost | Upkeep per month | Protects against |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Water reservoir | 600 | 10 | The Long Drought: drought losses halved for the whole colony |
| Fire brigade | 400 | 10 | Wildfires: fire no longer spreads between plots |
| Defence battery | 800 | 20 | Pirate Season: raids blocked as long as it is running |
| Quarantine station | 500 | 10 | The Plague Ship: if it is built before the crash, the epidemic is stopped |
| Meteor shield | 900 | 20 | Meteor strikes: half are shot down |
| Levee system | 500 | 10 | Floods on river plots halved |
| Grain reserve | 300 plus the Food to fill it | None | Famine: the store holds an extra month of Food |
| Market regulator | 400 | 10 | The Crystite Rush: land and Crystite prices can rise at most 20% per month |
| Colony observatory | 500 | 10 | Surprise: forecasts become public to everyone |
| Emergency generators | 600 | 10 | The Long Winter: the colony grid doesn't fail, and colonists' Energy demand doesn't double |
| Air purifier tower | 700 plus 4 Alloy | 10 | Pollution: the air gauge drops 3 points every month for the rest of the game |

- **Anyone can chip in.** Players can donate toward a project, and donations count toward their extra votes.
- **Politics around projects.** A levee mostly helps whoever owns river plots, so a Governor who farms the river has a reason to build it. Other players will notice and may vote them out.
- **Upkeep matters.** If the fund can't pay a project's upkeep, the project stops working until it can.

## Three roles

| Role | How you get it | Privileges |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Governor | Elected every 3 months | Sets the sales tax and the budget, proposes the month's law, starts colony projects and breaks ties |
| Sheriff | Elected every 3 months | Runs Security: guards plots, investigates sabotage and fines, enforces the laws, collects law taxes, and can open an inquiry into the Governor |
| Land Baron | Earned automatically by owning the most plots, so it can change hands any month | A second vote in the council and 10% off at the land auction |

**The split of power.** The Governor decides where the money goes and which rules to propose. The Sheriff decides how the colony's security is used and makes sure the rules are kept, including by the Governor. The Land Baron has no office, only weight. Because the Governor funds Security and the Sheriff spends it, the two have to work together, or fight in public.

### The Sheriff

The Sheriff is elected with the Governor every 3 months and acts in the planning phase, in secret on their phone unless stated.

| Power | How it works | Limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Guard plots | Puts guards on plots, any owner's. Sabotage against a guarded plot fails and the saboteur is revealed. | 1 plot, +1 for every 100 credits in the Security line. Guards are hidden until they catch someone. |
| Investigate | Picks one past sabotage, theft or broken deal. The culprit is revealed to everyone. | 1 a month, 2 when Security has 200 or more |
| Fine | Fines a revealed culprit or a law breaker. Half goes to the victim, half to the colony fund. | 50 to 300 credits; triple under the Sabotage ban law |
| Enforce laws | Inspects one player for a broken law (hoarding over the limit, a polluter in a green zone, an emission cap breach). A breach is fined and, for pollution, the plot is shut for a month. | 1 inspection a month |
| Collect law taxes | Pollution, land and windfall taxes are only collected from players the Sheriff has inspected that term, so the Sheriff chooses who feels them first. | Everyone must be inspected once per term |
| Escort the export ship | Protects the export ship and the store from pirate raids that month. | Needs 200 or more in Security |
| Inquiry into the Governor | Looks into the budget. If the Governor spent a line mostly for their own plots (a levee by their own river farms, say), the colony gets a no-confidence vote; a majority calls a snap election. | Once per term |

**Corruption is possible.** A player can bribe the Sheriff with a deal card ("don't inspect me this term"). A later Sheriff who investigates it reveals the bribe, and both lose reputation.

**Why you'd want the job.** Pay of 30 credits a month, a share of fines collected (10%), information nobody else has, and leverage: everyone wants to be on the Sheriff's good side.

### The Land Baron

Not elected: the title goes to whoever owns the most plots and can change hands any month. The Baron gets a second vote, 10% off at the land auction, opens the land auction (chooses which listed plot goes first) and breaks tied elections. It is the reward for expanding, and a target: the land tax and anti-monopoly act exist to rein the Baron in.

Nobody can be Governor and Sheriff at the same time. Each role held at the end of the game adds a small bonus to the final score. Beginner mode has no politics.

## Laws

Twelve laws can be proposed (the sales tax is not a law: the Governor sets it). Each one has a single setting the proposer picks, such as a tax rate or which good it applies to. That gives freedom without adding new rules. Every law counters a strategy that could otherwise dominate, so the council is how the table reins in a runaway leader.

When a law is proposed, the market report shows what it would do ("Food capped at 45; you hold 30 Food"), so everyone can see who wins and who loses before they vote.

**Market laws**

| Law | Proposer sets | Effect | Counters |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Price cap | A good and the cap level (1.2, 1.5 or 2 times the reference price) | That good can't trade above the cap, at the auction or in deals | Speculators squeezing a shortage |
| Export tax | 10%, 20% or 30% | That share of export ship sales goes into the colony fund | Exporters getting rich while the colony stays poor |
| Strategic reserve | 3 or 4 months of cover for Food and Water | The colony store holds more stock, which pushes prices up now and protects against crises later | Starvation crises |
| Anti-hoarding act | A storage limit per player per good: 30, 50 or 80 | Stock above the limit is sold to the store at half price | Cornering a market |
| Windfall tax | 25% or 50% | Profit on sales above twice the reference price is taxed into the colony fund | Big speculation wins |

**Land laws**

| Law | Proposer sets | Effect | Counters |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Land tax | 5, 10 or 20 credits per plot per month | Every owner pays per plot | The Land Baron's sprawl |
| Anti-monopoly act | 30%, 40% or 50% | Nobody may own more than that share of the plots producing one good; any excess must be sold in the land market | Dominating one good |
| Eminent domain | Which super tile | The colony takes that super tile for 3 months and shares its output equally | A runaway super tile owner |

**Order and fairness laws**

| Law | Proposer sets | Effect | Counters |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sabotage ban | Fine multiplier: 2 or 3 times | Detected sabotage pays a higher fine and loses more reputation | Constant sabotage |

The colony militia and the newcomer subsidy are now budget lines (Security and Welfare) instead of laws.

**Environment laws**

| Law | Proposer sets | Effect | Counters |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pollution tax | 10, 20 or 40 credits per air point caused | Each polluter pays for the pollution it causes, into the colony fund | Getting rich by fouling everyone's land |
| Emission cap | Air level: 40, 50 or 60 | No new Rare earth mines or smelters while the air gauge is above the level | A colony sliding into Collapse |
| Green zone | River, lake, or both | No polluters on those plots or next to them | Farmland and water ruined by industry |

**Rules for laws**

- A law stays in force until it is replaced or repealed. A repeal is a normal proposal.
- The same law can't be proposed again for 2 months after it fails, so nobody can spam the council.
- AI players vote by interest and persona. The Baron always fights a land tax, and the Good Neighbour backs the strategic reserve.
